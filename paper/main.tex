\title[A Semantic Knowledge Graph of the Rub al-Khali Metagenomic Expedition]{A Semantic Knowledge Graph of the Rub al-Khali Metagenomic Expedition}

\author*[1]{\fnm{First} \sur{Author}}\email{author@example.com}
\affil[1]{\orgdiv{Metagenomics Lab}, \orgname{Research University}, \orgaddress{\city{City}, \country{Saudi Arabia}}}

\abstract{Metagenomic exploration of extreme environments like the Rub al-Khali (Empty Quarter) desert generates heterogeneous data types, including geochemical profiles, environmental metadata, and sequencing records. Integrating these datasets to facilitate cross-domain analysis remains a significant challenge due to disparate formats and lack of semantic standardization. Here, we present a comprehensive Knowledge Graph (KG) of the Rub al-Khali expedition, representing 70 sampling sites and 2,535 soil samples. We utilized the Semanticscience Integrated Ontology (SIO) as a core framework to unify the resulting data---including more than 1.4 million taxon abundance observations with their provenance---covering DNA extraction, library preparation, and taxonomic abundances. The dataset is validated using Shape Expressions (ShEx) and competency questions executed via SPARQL. This semantic resource enables automated data integration and provides a foundation for multi-omic analysis of desert ecosystems.}

\keywords{Metagenomics, Knowledge Graph, Rub al-Khali, Semantic Web, SIO, Desert Microbiome}

\section{Introduction}\label{intro}

Extreme desert environments harbor unique microbial communities adapted to high solar radiation, desiccation, and nutrient limitation [1, 2]. The Rub al-Khali, or Empty Quarter, represents one of the largest continuous sand deserts globally, yet its microbial diversity and geochemical characteristics remain understudied [3]. Modern metagenomic expeditions in such regions produce vast quantities of data spanning field observations, laboratory measurements, and high-throughput sequencing [4].

Current approaches to managing these data often rely on isolated spreadsheets or specialized bioinformatics databases, which hinders integrated analysis across geochemical and biological dimensions [5]. Furthermore, the lack of formal semantics makes it difficult to maintain data provenance and ensure reproducibility [6]. To address these limitations, we developed a semantic Knowledge Graph that integrates all facets of the Rub al-Khali expedition. 

Here, we present a dataset that transforms raw field and laboratory data into a structured RDF (Resource Description Framework) format. We adopted a modular architecture separating the Terminological Box (TBox) from the Assertion Box (ABox), utilizing standardized ontologies to ensure interoperability. The resulting KG provides a queryable interface for the entire expedition, supporting complex biological and geochemical inquiries.

\section{Methods}\label{methods}

\subsection{Sample Collection}
We collected soil samples from 60 primary sites across the Empty Quarter of Saudi Arabia during five sampling campaigns: March 17-20, 2023; July 14, 2023; February 17-21, 2024; August 19-21, 2024; and October 2025. Due to extreme temperatures posing safety risks to researchers, the July 2023 campaign was limited to 8 sites. At each site, we collected samples from three distinct compartments: bulk surface soil (0 cm), bulk subsurface soil (5-10 cm depth, ``deep''), and subsurface soil near plant roots (5-10 cm depth, ``rhizosphere''). We collected three biological replicates from each compartment at every site by directly scooping soil into sterile 2 mL or 5 mL tubes. During field campaigns, we stored samples at -20\degree C, and then stored them at -80\degree C upon return to the laboratory until DNA extraction. For quality control, we included positive and negative controls for each sampling campaign. Positive controls consisted of three replicate tubes containing Zymo Mock Community Standard (ZymoBIOMICS, cat \#D6305), while negative controls consisted of three replicate tubes containing sterilized soil treated with UV light for 4 hours.

\subsection{DNA extraction and sequencing}
We extracted DNA from 0.9-1.2 g of soil using the PowerLyzer PowerSoil Kit (QIAGEN, catalog \#12855-50) or DNeasy PowerSoil Pro Kit (QIAGEN, catalog \#47014). We quantified DNA using the Qubit HS dsDNA kit (Qubit, catalog \#Q32854). For amplicon sequencing, we targeted the V3-V4 region of the 16S rRNA gene using Bakt\_341F and 806R primers [7, 8]. Libraries were sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Raw reads were processed using the nf-core/Ampliseq pipeline (v2.14.0) \cite{nfcore, ampliseq} and DADA2 \cite{dada2} to generate Amplicon Sequence Variants (ASVs).

\subsection{Knowledge Representation}
The Knowledge Graph is constructed using a modular architecture that strictly separates the Terminological Box (TBox) from the Assertion Box (ABox). The TBox defines the ontology schema using OWL 2 DL \cite{owl2}, while the ABox contains the instance data generated from the expedition.

\subsubsection{Ontology Engineering (TBox)}
We utilized the Semanticscience Integrated Ontology (SIO) \cite{sio} as the upper-level framework. Domain-specific classes were defined as subclasses of SIO entities. We present the core patterns below using Description Logic (DL) syntax.

\textbf{Measurement Pattern:} A quantitative measurement is modeled as a process that generates a value for a specific quality of a target entity.
\begin{align}
    \text{MeasurementProcess} & \sqsubseteq \text{Process} \\
    \text{Quality} & \sqsubseteq \text{Attribute} \\
    \text{Quantity} & \sqsubseteq \text{InformationContentEntity} \\
    \text{MeasurementProcess} & \sqsubseteq \exists \text{hasTarget}.\text{Entity} \sqcap \exists \text{hasOutput}.\text{Quantity} \\
    \text{Quantity} & \sqsubseteq \exists \text{hasValue}.\text{xsd:double} \sqcap \exists \text{hasUnit}.\text{Unit}
\end{align}

Corresponding SPARQL query for retrieving measurements:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
SELECT ?target ?value ?unit WHERE {
  ?process a :MeasurementProcess ;
           sio:hasTarget ?target ;
           sio:hasOutput ?quantity .
  ?quantity sio:hasValue ?value ;
            sio:hasUnit ?unit .
}
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Sampling Pattern:} A sampling event is a process that derives a sample from a feature of interest (the site).
\begin{align}
    \text{SamplingProcess} & \sqsubseteq \text{Process} \\
    \text{Sample} & \sqsubseteq \text{MaterialEntity} \\
    \text{SamplingProcess} & \sqsubseteq \exists \text{hasTarget}.\text{Site} \sqcap \exists \text{hasOutput}.\text{Sample} \\
    \text{Sample} & \sqsubseteq \exists \text{isDerivedFrom}.\text{Site}
\end{align}

Corresponding SPARQL query for sample provenance:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
SELECT ?sample ?site WHERE {
  ?process a :SamplingProcess ;
           sio:hasTarget ?site ;
           sio:hasOutput ?sample .
  ?sample sio:isDerivedFrom ?site .
}
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Taxonomic Abundance Pattern:} Taxonomic profiles are represented as collections of taxon observations, each with an associated relative or absolute abundance.
\begin{align}
    \text{TaxonAbundance} & \sqsubseteq \text{Quantity} \\
    \text{TaxonAbundance} & \sqsubseteq \exists \text{isAttributeOf}.\text{Taxon} \sqcap \exists \text{hasValue}.\text{xsd:double}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Data Generation (ABox)}
The ABox is instantiated via an automated ETL pipeline that maps raw tabular data to the TBox definitions. We assert facts about specific individuals using DL assertion axioms. For example, to assert that a specific sampling process $p_{10}$ targeted site $s_{10}$ and produced sample $m_{10}$, we use:
\begin{align}
    \text{SamplingProcess}(p_{10}) \\
    \text{Site}(s_{10}) \\
    \text{Sample}(m_{10}) \\
    \text{hasTarget}(p_{10}, s_{10}) \\
    \text{hasOutput}(p_{10}, m_{10}) \\
    \text{isDerivedFrom}(m_{10}, s_{10})
\end{align}
This rigorous formalization ensures that the generated RDF graph remains consistent with the ontological schema.

\subsubsection{RDF Implementation}
The central pattern used for quantitative data is the SIO Measurement Pattern, which decomposes a measurement into four related entities: the Measurement Process, the Target entity, the Quality being measured, and the resulting Quantity. For example, a DNA concentration measurement is represented as follows:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, caption=Turtle representation of a DNA concentration measurement.]
:dna_ext_proc_10Dr1 a rak:RAK_0000041 ; # DNA Extraction Process
    sio:SIO_000291 :sample_10Dr1 ;      # has target
    sio:SIO_000229 :dna_ext_10Dr1 .      # has output

:dna_ext_10Dr1 a rak:RAK_0000040 ;       # DNA Extract
    sio:SIO_000008 :dna_conc_qual_10Dr1 . # has quality

:dna_conc_qual_10Dr1 a obo:PATO_0000033 ; # concentration
    sio:SIO_000215 :dna_conc_qty_10Dr1 .  # has measurement value

:dna_conc_qty_10Dr1 a sio:SIO_000052 ;    # quantity
    sio:SIO_000300 "52.3"^^xsd:double ;   # has value
    sio:SIO_000221 obo:UO_0000275 .       # has unit (ng/uL)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{Data Integration and Materialization}
Data transformation is performed incrementally. The pipeline first generates the site ontology, followed by samples, measurements, geochemical data (XRF), and sequencing records (SRA). We utilized the OpenLink Virtuoso triple store for data hosting. During the loading process, we materialized inferred triples using OWL-Horst reasoning to optimize query performance, specifically for property chains and inverse relationships.

\section{Data Records}\label{records}

The Rub al-Khali Knowledge Graph integrates diverse data types into a unified semantic framework. We describe the specific modeling patterns and records for each domain below.

\subsection{Sampling Sites and Biomes}
We identified 70 sampling sites.
\textbf{Pattern:} Sites are instances of \texttt{rak:RAK\_0000002} (Sampling Site). We utilize GeoSPARQL for spatial representation and ENVO for biome classification via OWL restrictions.
\textbf{Validation:} Confirmed by \texttt{sites.shex} which enforces the presence of \texttt{geo:asWKT} literals.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, caption=Turtle representation of a Sampling Site.]
:site_10 a rak:RAK_0000002 ;
    rdfs:label "Site 10" ;
    geo:asWKT "POINT(50.123 20.456)"^^geo:wktLiteral ;
    a [ a owl:Restriction ;
        owl:onProperty rak:RAK_2000001 ; # hasBiome
        owl:someValuesFrom obo:ENVO_00000192 ] . # Desert Biome
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Soil Samples and Provenance}
The dataset contains 2,535 soil samples.
\textbf{Pattern:} We use the SIO Collection pattern. A \texttt{SamplingProcess} generates a \texttt{Collection} which contains individual \texttt{Sample} replicates.
\textbf{Validation:} \texttt{samples.shex} ensures every sample is linked to a site via \texttt{sio:isDerivedFrom}.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, caption=Turtle representation of Sample Provenance.]
:sampling_proc_10 a rak:RAK_0000024 ; # Sampling Process
    sio:SIO_000291 :site_10 ;        # has target
    sio:SIO_000229 :collection_10 .  # has output

:collection_10 a sio:SIO_001419 ;    # Collection
    sio:SIO_000059 :sample_10Dr1 .   # has member

:sample_10Dr1 a rak:RAK_0000021 ;    # Soil Sample
    sio:SIO_000244 :site_10 .        # is derived from
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{DNA Extracts and Sequencing Libraries}
We recorded 2,410 DNA extracts and 1,242 sequencing libraries.
\textbf{Pattern:} DNA extraction is modeled as a process transforming a Soil Sample input into a DNA Extract output. Library preparation further transforms this extract into a Sequencing Library, enriched with primer metadata.
\textbf{Validation:} \texttt{dna.shex} and \texttt{sra.shex} validate the inputs and outputs of these respective processes.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, caption=Turtle representation of Library Prep.]
:lib_prep_proc_10 a rak:RAK_0000065 ; # Lib Prep Process
    sio:SIO_000230 :dna_extract_10 ;  # has input
    sio:SIO_000229 :lib_10 ;          # has output
    rak:RAK_2000018 :primer_fwd ;     # uses fwd primer
    rak:RAK_2000019 :primer_rev .     # uses rev primer
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Geochemical Data (XRF)}
XRF profiles provide elemental composition for samples.
\textbf{Pattern:} Detailed measurement values are linked to the sample via a \texttt{Quality} (e.g., Iron Concentration).
\textbf{Validation:} \texttt{xrf.shex} requires values to have units (percent) and links to ChEBI-typed qualities.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, caption=Turtle representation of XRF Data.]
:iron_conc_qual a rak:RAK_0000030 ;   # Iron Concentration
    sio:SIO_000011 :sample_10Dr1 ;    # attribute of sample
    sio:SIO_000215 :iron_value .      # measurement value

:iron_value a sio:SIO_000052 ;
    rak:RAK_2000012 "4.5"^^xsd:double ; # numeric value
    sio:SIO_000221 obo:UO_0000187 .     # unit (percent)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{External Ontology Mappings}
To ensure semantic interoperability, we mapped internal data codes to standardized external ontologies.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Chemical Entities (ChEBI):} All 56 XRF analytes are mapped to persistent ChEBI identifiers. For example, Iron (Fe) is mapped to \texttt{obo:CHEBI\_18248}, and Silicon Dioxide ($SiO_2$) to \texttt{obo:CHEBI\_30563}.
    \item \textbf{Taxonomy (NCBI):} We mapped 21,659 local taxonomic assignments to NCBI Taxonomy IRIs. Where direct matches were unavailable (e.g., for novel "KB1 group"), we maintained local RAK identifiers (`is\_rak: true`) while rooting them correctly within the NCBI hierarchy (e.g., under \textit{Bacteria}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Technical Validation}\label{valid}

\subsection{Shape Expressions (ShEx) Validation}
We validated the structural integrity of the RDF graph using domain-specific Shape Expressions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sites}: \texttt{sites.shex} verifies GeoSPARQL compliance.
    \item \textbf{Samples}: \texttt{samples.shex} ensures provenance links to parent sites.
    \item \textbf{Sequencing}: \texttt{sra.shex} and \texttt{dna.shex} validate the experimental lineage from extract to library.
    \item \textbf{XRF}: \texttt{xrf.shex} checks the datatype properties of geochemical measurements.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Competency Questions}
We evaluated the utility of the Knowledge Graph using 9 competency questions implemented as SPARQL queries. Each query targets specific data domains:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sites (Query 2, 3)}: "List all Sampling Sites and their Coordinates" and "List all Biomes associated with Sites".
    \item \textbf{Geochemistry (Query 1, 4)}: "Retrieve Field XRF Measurement for Site 10" and "Retrieve Light Elements Percentages".
    \item \textbf{DNA \& Sequencing (Query 5, 6)}: "Retrieve DNA Extraction Data" and "Trace Provenance from Soil Sample to Sequencing Run".
    \item \textbf{Taxonomy \& QC (Query 7, 8, 9)}: "Retrieve Taxonomic Composition", "Reproduce Taxon Table", and "Query Sequencing QC Metrics".
\end{itemize}
We verified the query results against the original structured input files to ensure 100\% accuracy in the transformation process.

\subsection{Web-based Visualization}
We deployed a React-based frontend that interacts directly with the SPARQL endpoint. This application serves as a functional validation tool, allowing researchers to explore the data through interactive maps and taxonomic dashboards without intermediate database layers. The consistency between the visualized data and the underlying KG confirms the accessibility and correctness of the semantic mappings.

\subsection{Data Access via SPARQL}
To facilitate large-scale analysis, the entire dataset can be retrieved using generalized SPARQL queries. Below we provide examples for exporting the core geochemical and biological datasets.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, caption=SPARQL query to retrieve the entire XRF geochemical dataset.]
SELECT ?sampleLabel ?analyte ?concentration ?unitLabel
WHERE {
  ?sample a rak:RAK_0000021 ; rdfs:label ?sampleLabel .
  ?sample sio:SIO_000008 ?quality .
  ?quality sio:SIO_000215 ?value .
  ?value rak:RAK_2000012 ?concentration . 
  ?value sio:SIO_000221 ?unit ; rdfs:label ?unitLabel .
  ?quality a ?qualityClass . ?qualityClass rdfs:label ?analyte .
  FILTER(?qualityClass != rak:RAK_0000029) 
}
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL, caption=SPARQL query to retrieve the entire Taxon Abundance table.]
SELECT ?run ?taxonLabel ?count ?relativeAbundance
WHERE {
  ?proc a rak:RAK_0000071 ; sio:SIO_000230 ?fastq ;
        sio:SIO_000229 ?dsAbs , ?dsRel .
  ?dsAbs a rak:RAK_0000074 . ?dsRel a rak:RAK_0000075 .
  ?dsAbs sio:SIO_000059 ?valAbs . ?valAbs rak:RAK_2000026 ?count .
  ?valAbs sio:SIO_000215 ?qualAbs . ?qualAbs sio:SIO_000011 ?taxon .
  ?taxon rdfs:label ?taxonLabel .
  ?qualRel sio:SIO_000011 ?taxon ; a rak:RAK_0000072 ;
           sio:SIO_000214 ?valRel . ?valRel rak:RAK_2000020 ?relativeAbundance .
  FILTER(?taxon != ?fastq)
}
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Usage Notes}\label{usage}
The Knowledge Graph is accessible via a public SPARQL endpoint at \url{http://localhost:8895/sparql} (local deployment) or through the project's web interface. Users can export specific subgraphs in Turtle or JSON-LD formats for downstream analysis. We recommend using the provided prefixes (e.g., \texttt{rak:}, \texttt{sio:}) to facilitate query construction.
