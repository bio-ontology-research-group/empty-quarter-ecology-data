\section{Methods}\label{methods}

\subsection{Study description}

\subsubsection{Study area}
We conducted five sampling campaigns along a transect across the Rub' al-Khali starting from the western escarpment (19.15\degree N, 45.17\degree
E) to the eastern Saudi Arabian border with Oman (20.71\degree N,
54.73\degree E). The 60 primary sampling sites span 1,015~km in a straight line and 1,043-km along the transect, calculated by summing great-circle distances between successive sites using an Earth radius of 6,371~km.
Figure~\ref{fig:transect} shows the site coordinates and their altitudes.

In addition to the 60 primary sites, the survey included 10 named locations such as wells, a hot spring, and an oil-spill site. These locations were not part of the repeated sampling campaign. Comparisons across campaigns therefore focus on the 60 primary sites, and metadata links each observation to its sampling site, campaign, and sample.


\subsubsection{Sampling design}
To capture temporal variability, we conducted campaigns across
different seasons: late winter 2023 (Trip 1), summer
2023 (Trip 2, truncated to 8 sites due to extreme temperatures), winter 2024 (Trip 3), summer 2024 (Trip 4), and autumn 2025 (Trip 5). Table~\ref{tab:env_summary} summarizes sites visited during each campaign.

At each site, we sampled three soil compartments: bulk surface soil, bulk subsurface soil (nominal 5--10 cm depth), and subsurface soil collected near roots (nominal 5--10 cm depth, hereafter ``root-adjacent''). We collected three field replicates per compartment using sterile tubes and sterile plastic spoons, or three tubes taped together. Exact tube diameters, surface sampling depth, individual bulk subsurface depths and distance from roots were unrecorded. We stored samples at $-20$\degree C in the field
in an Engel MR040F portable freezer, and transferred samples to an $-80$\degree C freezer in the laboratory at the end of each campaign. Table~\ref{tab:sample_coverage} summarizes soil samples collected by campaign and compartment.

\subsubsection{Field observations}
We recorded air temperature, atmospheric pressure, and relative humidity during site visits, although coverage varied between campaigns (Table~\ref{tab:env_summary}. Temperature is reported in degrees Celsius, pressure in pascals, and relative humidity in percent. The field logs record pressure in hectopascals (millibars); we multiplied these values by 100 and assert them in pascals (\texttt{obo:UO\_0000110}) in the graph. Trip~2 recorded only temperature, with eight measurements spanning 34.5--41.9\degree C. Field observations entered the dataset only when their site identity and measurement range could be established. Additionally, field researchers classified each site by an observed environmental feature (\textit{e.g.} sand dune, saline pan, gravel), and we mapped these features to ENVO \cite{envo}.

\subsection{Sample processing}
\subsubsection{DNA extraction}
We extracted genomic DNA from 0.9--1.2~g of soil using either the
QIAGEN PowerLyzer PowerSoil Kit (catalogue no.\ 12855-50) or the QIAGEN
DNeasy PowerSoil Pro Kit (catalogue no.\ 47014). The extraction kit and method were recorded for samples when available. Frozen soil samples were thawed on ice and processed immediately. Before homogenization, we incubated samples at 65\degree C for 10~min with agitation at 1,200~rpm, then homogenized using a PowerLyzer 24 Homogenizer at 4,000~rpm for one 45-second cycle. The remaining purification steps were performed either
manually or using a QIAcube Connect instrument, following the
manufacturer's protocol. We eluted DNA in 50~\textmu L of the elution
buffer supplied with each kit, and stored extracted DNA in $-20$\degree C.

We quantified extracted DNA using the Qubit dsDNA High Sensitivity
assay, with fluorescence measured using a Varioskan LUX multimode
plate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific). We recorded extract concentrations and processing information for each sample.

\subsubsection{16S rRNA amplification}
We amplified the V3--V4 region of the bacterial 16S rRNA gene using
the Bakt\_341F (\texttt{CCTACGGGNGGCWGCAG}) and Bakt\_785R
(\texttt{GACTACHVGGGTATCTAATCC}) primers \cite{klindworth2013primers},
both carrying Illumina overhang adapters. We performed first-round PCR using TaKaRa Ex Taq DNA Polymerase (catalogue no.\
RR001B). Each 50-\textmu L reaction contained
5~\textmu L of 10$\times$ buffer with Mg$^{2+}$, 4~\textmu L of dNTP mix
(2.5~mM each), 2~\textmu L of each primer (10~\textmu M), 0.25~\textmu L of polymerase (5~U/\textmu L), 5~\textmu L of DNA and 31.75~\textmu L of
nuclease-free water, giving final concentrations of 0.4~\textmu M of each
primer, 0.2~mM of each dNTP and 1.25~U of polymerase. Cycling conditions
were 95\degree C for 3~min; 35 cycles of 95\degree C for 10~s, 65\degree C
for 30~s and 72\degree C for 1~min; and a final extension at 75\degree C for 5~min.

We checked PCR product on a 1\% agarose gel for an expected amplicon size of 500~bp. We re-amplified samples without a visible product with 8~\textmu L and then 12~\textmu L of DNA, and we set DNA input by volume because most extracts had low or undetectable concentrations. Samples that failed all three attempts were recorded as failed.

\subsubsection{Indexing and sequencing}
We added dual indices and Illumina sequencing adapters in a
second-round PCR using the QIAGEN Multiplex PCR Kit (catalogue no.\
206143), following the Illumina 16S Metagenomic Sequencing Library
Preparation protocol \cite{illumina16S}: initial activation at
94\degree C for 15~min; 8 cycles of 94\degree C for 30~s, 55\degree C
for 30~s and 72\degree C for 30~s; final extension at 72\degree C for
5~min.

We purified indexed libraries using Agencourt AMPure XP beads (0.8:1 bead to sample volume), quantified with the Qubit dsDNA High Sensitivity assay, and checked for fragment size on an Agilent 4200 TapeStation (D5000 ScreenTape). We pooled libraries in equimolar amounts, calculating molarity from concentration and fragment size. Pools were sequenced on an Ollumina NovaSeq~6000 with an SP flow cell and a 500-cycle kit ($2 \times 250$~bp paired-end reads). Library preparation was performed in the PI's laboratory and sequencing at the KAUST Bioscience Core Lab.

\subsubsection{Control samples}
We followed recommended guidelines for reporting contamination in low biomass samples by having controls for differnent study stages \cite{fierer2025controls} (Table~\ref{tab:controls}). To control for contamination introduced during DNA extraction, we included extraction blanks with nuclease-free water as input instead of soil, and processed them the same way as soil samples. Trips~1--3 used one extraction blank per extraction kit, and Trips~1--2 included shared extraction days; Trips~4--5 used one blank per trip-specific extraction-day batch. For Trip~4, we sequenced six extraction blanks (SEB, EB1--EB5); the blanks of three of the nine extraction batches were not sequenced. For Trip~5, we sequenced 18 extraction blanks (EB1--EB18), 17 of which have confirmed batch links to 217 biological profiles.

We included two types of PCR negative controls. No-template controls (NTCs) contained PCR reagents and nuclease-free water instead of DNA, and reagent-only controls per amplification plate that contained reagents but neither water nor DNA. Reagent-only controls had low or undetectable DNA concentrations and TapeStation profiles of only adapter dimers, so they were not sequenced. We sequenced six NTCs that were prepared with Trip~5 samples.

We also included positive standards to assess how well expected genera were recovered. During Trips 1 and 2 we used the ZymoBIOMICS HMW DNA Standard (D6322), which contained purified DNA and was added at the PCR step. D6322 contains seven bacterial genomes (14\% of genomic DNA each) and one yeast (2\%). During Trip~3 we used the ZymoBIOMICS Microbial Community Standard (D6300) which contains whole cells and was therefore included from the extraction step. D6300 contains eight
bacteria (12\% of genomic DNA each) and two yeasts (2\% each). These
proportions are the manufacturer's values. Two of each type of positive standard were amplified and sequenced.

\subsubsection{PMA-treated samples}
DNA extracted from soil includes DNA from intact cells as well as extracellular DNA and DNA from dead or damaged cells \cite{carini2016relic}. Propidium monoazide (PMA) is a dye that enters only cells with damaged membranes and binds to free DNA, and prevents amplification by PCR when exposed to light. We compare PMA-treated and untreated aliquots of the same soil samples to estimate how much of the 16S rRNA gene signal comes from cells with intact membranes. We applied this treatment to a subset of Trip~5 samples.

The PMA dataset includes three soil samples collected from campsites during Trip~5. We collected root-adjacent soil from campsite C1, and root-adjacent and surface soil from campsite C2. From each collection tube, we took three samples and suspended each in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH~7.4) at a soil-to-buffer ratio of 1:10 (w/v). The three samples from each collection tube are technical replicates of the treament and not independent field samples. We split each sample into a treated aliquot that included 50~\textmu M PMAxx, and an untreated aliquot, giving nine treated/untreated pairs.

We subjected both aliquots to the same procedure. We kept the aliquots in the dark for 10~min to allow the dye to enter the cells, and then exposed them to 450 nm light for 35~min at a distance of 15--20~cm from the light source, and turned them every 15~min to ensure even exposure. We stored aliquots at $-20$\degree C, and extracted DNA from 860~\textmu L of each aliquots with the PowerSoil Pro kit.


\subsubsection{Data processing}
We processed raw amplicon reads with nf-core/ampliseq (v2.14.0)
\cite{nfcore, ampliseq}, using DADA2 \cite{dada2} for error
correction and denoising, and SILVA (v138.2) \cite{silva} for taxonomic assignment. This resulted in 351,472 amplicon sequence variants (ASVs), and included 1,247 biological samples and 24 control samples. Quality filtering excludes the 24 control profiles and ten biological profiles with fewer than 1,000 reads, including one \texttt{T1Dr1} run with 934 reads. This leaves 1,237 profiles, including 1,227 profiles from sites~1--60, for the companion ecology analysis; the complete table is distributed unfiltered. The container digest and full denoising parameters are included in the repository.

We predicted gene family and pathway abundances from the 16S rRNA data with PICRUSt2 v2.4.1 \cite{picrust2}. Two executions used the Trips~1--4 ampliseq output and the Trip~5 output (330,830 ASVs). KEGG Orthology (KO) prediction used the phylogenetic independent contrasts method, edge exponent 0.5, and random seed 100. Enzyme Comission (EC) and KO abundances required 100 reads, and pathway abundances were inferred from the EC predictions.

\subsection{Soil Measurements}
\subsubsection{Soil composition (XRF)}\label{methods:xrf}
We measured soil elemental composition by X-ray fluorescence (XRF) using an Olympus Vanta VMR-XXX-G3-E handheld analyser (serial no.\ 841307), both in the field during Trip~5 and in the laborator on archived soil samples from all campaigns. The field log contains 106 entries across 59 sites. For field measurements, we used the instrument in Geochem (3-Beam) mode with NORMAL settings, and retained 71 complete measurement sessions from 58 sites, including repeated sessions at nine sites. In the laboratory, we analyzed 725 samples with the instrument in Fast Mode with the Fast Screening-He8mm setting.  Elemental concentrations and their oxide equivalents are recorded as separate variables and are not asssigned units.

To assess agreement between field and laboratory measurements, we compared the median of repeated field sessions at each site with the corresponding Trip~5 laboratory measurements at the 58 sites measured using both methods. This comparison assesses agreement in rankings rather than absolute concentrations, and we report the correlations in Technical Validation.

We cross-referenced the 93 reported elements against ChEBI r247 (3 December 2025) and PubChem properties (retrieved 23 July 2026) \cite{chebi,pubchem}. We identified corresponding ChEBI entries for 79 variables and PubChem entries for 91 entries. We verified identifier validity, names, chemical formulae, charge, and whether each variable represented an element or an oxide.

\subsubsection{Soil pH}\label{methods:ph}
We measured the pH of archived soil samples from all five campaigns. We sieved dried soil to below 2~mm and mixed 1~g of soil with 2.5~mL of 0.01~M CaCl$_2$. We mixed each suspension for 30~min in a HulaMixer and let it stand for another 30~min at room temperature before measuring pH.

We measured pH using an accumet AB315 meter with a Fisher Scientific electrode (cat. no.~13-620-112) which we calibrated at the start of each session, and we recorded the electrode slop and the reading of the pH~10 buffer for every session. We rinsed the electrode with Milli-Q water between measurements. We oretained 712 measurements with a reocrded electrode slop between 95\% and 102\% and a pH~10 buffer reading within 0.1 units of its nominal value (Table~\ref{tab:ph_coverage}). All 156 admitted Trip~4 pH measurements used archived field replicate~2. Each admitted measurement is linked to its physical specimen and retains its original assay identifier. The ecology analysis uses the same measurement values.

\subsection{Climate data}
We retrieved historical climate data for each sampling site from the Open-Meteo archive API \cite{openmeteo} using geographic coordinates. We obtained both monthly and daily climate observations. The monthly dataset contains 12,936 values that cover temperature, total precipitation, rainfall, and mean relative humiditiy from January 2022 to January 2026. The daily dataset covers 1~January 2022 to 1~February 2026 and covers temperature, total precipitation, and rainfall. The monthly and daily products come from two separately configured acquisitions; the monthly acquisition ends on 20~January 2026, so January 2026 is a partial month in the monthly data.

We also retrieved daily prepipitation data from NASA POWER PRECTOTCORR for sites 1--60 from 1 January 2022 to 31 December 2025, which yielded 87,660 records.

\subsection{Shotgun metagenomics and genome catalogue}\label{methods:shotgun}

Shotgun metagenomic sequencing complements the amplicon profiles with
genome assemblies and gene annotations from the same sampling campaigns.
The read-bearing library inventory contains 551 libraries across 61 site
codes: 210 from Trip~1, 37 from Trip~2, 128 from Trip~3, 85 from Trip~4
and 91 from Trip~5. Library metadata records the campaign, source site,
soil compartment and replicate. The libraries were sequenced as 554
paired-end runs, 221 on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 and 333 on an Illumina
NovaSeq X; three libraries were sequenced on both instruments. Libraries
for 81 runs were prepared with the NEBNext Ultra II DNA Library Prep Kit
and for 473 runs with the QIAseq FX DNA Library UDI Kit. The shotgun study is registered at ENA
under \texttt{PRJEB125153} (secondary accession \texttt{ERP204894}).

Genome reconstruction combined MEGAHIT v1.2.9 assembly \cite{megahit} with single-sample and
self-supervised SemiBin2 v2.2.1 binning \cite{semibin2}, followed by DAS Tool
v1.1.7 refinement \cite{dastool}. MEGAHIT produced contigs of at least
500~bp for 549 of the 551 libraries; libraries 51Sr1 and F5Dr1 yielded no
contigs. CheckM2 v1.1.0 \cite{checkm2} estimated completeness and contamination for the
refined bins, and 12,434 refined bins reached at least 50\% completeness
with at most 10\% contamination. We dereplicated the bins with dRep \cite{drep}, using
CheckM2 quality estimates, a minimum completeness of 50\%, a maximum
contamination of 10\%, and a minimum genome length of 50,000~bp.
The recorded clustering parameters specify 90\% primary ANI and
95\% secondary ANI, with fastANI for secondary comparisons and a minimum
alignment coverage of 0.1 using dRep's \texttt{larger} coverage mode.
The resulting catalogue contains 5,438 representative identifiers.
The provenance table links each identifier to its source library,
binning strategy and bin, and records quality estimates from the refined-bin
CheckM2 report.

Taxonomic assignments for the refined representatives used GTDB-Tk
v2.7.2 with GTDB release R232 \cite{gtdb}. The two native classification
tables contain 4,847 bacterial and 591 archaeal representatives, and
their genome identifiers match the representative catalogue exactly.
We detected rRNA genes with barrnap v0.9 and tRNA genes with tRNAscan-SE
v2.0.12 \cite{trnascan}, and assigned each representative a quality tier
following the MIMAG standard \cite{mimag}. Of the 5,438 representatives,
126 meet the high-quality criteria, which require more than 90\%
completeness, less than 5\% contamination, the 23S, 16S and 5S rRNA genes
and tRNAs for at least 18 of the 20 amino acids. Another 5,311 meet the
medium-quality criteria of at least 50\% completeness and less than 10\%
contamination. The remaining representative,
\texttt{48Dr2\_\_self\_SemiBin\_25}, has 10\% contamination.

We annotated the genes of each representative with Bakta v1.12.0
\cite{bakta} and its full database v6.0. Bakta predicts coding sequences,
rRNA and tRNA genes and translates the coding sequences into proteins.
eggNOG-mapper v2.1.13 \cite{eggnog} then searched these proteins against
the eggNOG v5.0.2 database with DIAMOND v2.0.15 and assigned KEGG
orthologs (KOs), CAZy families, Pfam domains and preferred gene names.
For each genome, we counted the predicted proteins, the proteins with an
eggNOG assignment and the proteins with at least one KO. We also counted,
for each genome, the proteins that carry each KO, CAZy family, Pfam domain
and preferred name. These feature tables list only the genome--feature
pairs that occur in the annotations.

To estimate the coverage of each representative in each library, we
mapped the reads of all 551 libraries to a single reference that contains
every contig of the 5,438 representatives. We prefixed each contig
identifier with its genome identifier, so that contigs with the same
assembler-assigned name in different genomes remain distinct. Bowtie2
v2.5.1 \cite{bowtie2} aligned the first 200 million read pairs of each
library in \texttt{--very-sensitive} mode and retained aligned reads only;
SAMtools v1.16.1 \cite{samtools} sorted the alignments. CoverM v0.6.1
\cite{coverm} in genome mode counted reads aligned with at least 95\%
identity and reported, for every genome, the mean depth, the covered
fraction, the trimmed mean depth, the number of mapped reads and the
genome length.

The ecology comparison uses a separate 991-genome catalogue and
150 library profiles from Trips~1--3 (58, 15 and 77 libraries,
respectively). Its CoverM profiles \cite{coverm} report genome-relative abundance,
and eggNOG-mapper v2.1.12 \cite{eggnog} annotations yield a
990-genome KO copy-number matrix after intersecting the annotated genomes
with the abundance profiles. These inputs and the six source tables for
the MAG-derived versus PICRUSt2 comparison retain their analytical-subset
provenance.

\subsection{Reproducible workflow}
A Nextflow \cite{di2017nextflow} workflow generates the knowledge graph modules, runs the validation checks, and computes the statistics reported in this manuscript. Configuration profiles are available for running the workflow locally or a on a computing cluster. Sample processing (Section~\ref{methods}) was run seperately with its own parameters and software environments.

The workflow produces each versioned release of the knowledge graph by generating the data modules, comparing their checksums with those listed in the release manifest, loading them into a new database, and checking the results of test queries against the source data. The public service is switched to the new release only after these checks pass. The lease manifests list the fixed input files and the generated modules along with with their checksums.
