\subsection{Sampling design and field observations}

Five sampling campaigns covered a transect across the Rub' al-Khali starting from the western escarpment (19.15\degree N, 45.17\degree
E) to the eastern Saudi Arabian border with Oman (20.71\degree N,
54.73\degree E). The 60 primary sampling sites span 1,015~km in a straight line and 1,043-km along the transect, calculated by summing great-circle distances between successive sites using an Earth radius of 6,371~km.
Figure~\ref{fig:transect} shows the site coordinates and their altitudes.

In addition to the 60 primary sites, the survey included 10 named locations such as wells, a hot spring, and an oil-spill site. These locations were not part of the repeated sampling campaign. Comparisons across campaigns therefore focus on the 60 primary sites, and metadata links each observation to its sampling site, campaign, and sample.


The campaigns span different seasons: late winter 2023 (Trip 1), summer
2023 (Trip 2, truncated to 8 sites due to extreme temperatures), winter 2024 (Trip 3), summer 2024 (Trip 4), and autumn 2025 (Trip 5). Table~\ref{tab:env_summary} summarizes sites visited during each campaign.

At each site, we sampled three soil compartments: bulk surface soil, bulk subsurface soil (nominal 5--10 cm depth), and subsurface soil collected near roots (nominal 5--10 cm depth, hereafter ``root-adjacent''). We collected three field replicates per compartment using sterile tubes and sterile plastic spoons, or three tubes taped together. Exact tube diameters, surface sampling depth, individual bulk subsurface depths and distance from roots were unrecorded. We stored samples at $-20$\degree C in the field
in an Engel MR040F portable freezer, and transferred samples to an $-80$\degree C freezer in the laboratory at the end of each campaign. Table~\ref{tab:sample_coverage} summarizes soil samples collected by campaign and compartment.

\subsubsection{Field observations}
We recorded air temperature, atmospheric pressure, and relative humidity during site visits, although coverage varied between campaigns (Table~\ref{tab:env_summary}). The field logs record pressure in hectopascals (millibars); we multiplied these values by 100 and assert them in pascals (\texttt{obo:UO\_0000110}) in the graph. Trip~2 recorded only temperature, with eight measurements spanning 34.5--41.9\degree C. Field observations entered the dataset only when their site identity and measurement range could be established. \subsection{DNA extraction and amplicon sequencing}
Genomic DNA was extracted from 0.9--1.2~g of soil using either the
QIAGEN PowerLyzer PowerSoil Kit (catalogue no.\ 12855-50) or the QIAGEN
DNeasy PowerSoil Pro Kit (catalogue no.\ 47014). The extraction kit and method were recorded for samples when available. Frozen soil samples were thawed on ice and processed immediately. Before homogenization, samples were incubated at 65\degree C for 10~min with agitation at 1,200~rpm, then homogenized using a PowerLyzer 24 Homogenizer at 4,000~rpm for one 45-second cycle. The remaining purification steps were performed either
manually or using a QIAcube Connect instrument, following the
manufacturer's protocol. DNA was eluted in 50~\textmu L of the elution
buffer supplied with each kit, and stored at $-20$\degree C.

Extracted DNA was quantified using the Qubit dsDNA High Sensitivity
assay, with fluorescence measured using a Varioskan LUX multimode
plate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Extract concentrations and processing information were recorded per sample.

The V3--V4 region of the bacterial 16S rRNA gene was amplified using
the Bakt\_341F (\texttt{CCTACGGGNGGCWGCAG}) and Bakt\_785R
(\texttt{GACTACHVGGGTATCTAATCC}) primers \cite{klindworth2013primers},
both carrying Illumina overhang adapters. First-round PCR used TaKaRa Ex Taq DNA Polymerase (catalogue no.\
RR001B). Each 50-\textmu L reaction contained
5~\textmu L of 10$\times$ buffer with Mg$^{2+}$, 4~\textmu L of dNTP mix
(2.5~mM each), 2~\textmu L of each primer (10~\textmu M), 0.25~\textmu L of polymerase (5~U/\textmu L), 5~\textmu L of DNA and 31.75~\textmu L of
nuclease-free water, giving final concentrations of 0.4~\textmu M of each
primer, 0.2~mM of each dNTP and 1.25~U of polymerase. Cycling conditions
were 95\degree C for 3~min; 35 cycles of 95\degree C for 10~s, 65\degree C
for 30~s and 72\degree C for 1~min; and a final extension at 75\degree C for 5~min.

PCR products were checked on a 1\% agarose gel for an expected amplicon size of 500~bp. Samples without a visible product were re-amplified with 8~\textmu L and then 12~\textmu L of DNA, with DNA input set by volume because most extracts had low or undetectable concentrations. Samples that failed all three attempts were recorded as failed.

Dual indices and Illumina sequencing adapters were added in a
second-round PCR using the QIAGEN Multiplex PCR Kit (catalogue no.\
206143), following the Illumina 16S Metagenomic Sequencing Library
Preparation protocol \cite{illumina16S}: initial activation at
94\degree C for 15~min; 8 cycles of 94\degree C for 30~s, 55\degree C
for 30~s and 72\degree C for 30~s; final extension at 72\degree C for
5~min.

Indexed libraries were purified using Agencourt AMPure XP beads (0.8:1 bead to sample volume), quantified with the Qubit dsDNA High Sensitivity assay, and checked for fragment size on an Agilent 4200 TapeStation (D5000 ScreenTape). Libraries were pooled in equimolar amounts, calculating molarity from concentration and fragment size. Pools were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq~6000 with an SP flow cell and a 500-cycle kit ($2 \times 250$~bp paired-end reads). Library preparation was performed in the research laboratory and sequencing at the KAUST Bioscience Core Lab.

\subsection{Laboratory controls and paired PMA treatments}


Controls cover different processing stages, following reporting guidance for low-biomass samples \cite{fierer2025controls} (Table~\ref{tab:controls}). To control for contamination introduced during DNA extraction, extraction blanks were included with nuclease-free water as input instead of soil, and processed in the same way as soil samples. Trips~1--3 used one extraction blank per extraction kit, and Trips~1--2 included shared extraction days; Trips~4--5 used one blank per trip-specific extraction-day batch. For Trip~4, we sequenced six extraction blanks (SEB, EB1--EB5); the blanks of three of the nine extraction batches were not sequenced. For Trip~5, we sequenced 18 extraction blanks (EB1--EB18), 17 of which have confirmed batch links to 217 biological profiles, whereas EB18 has no frozen extraction-day or co-extracted-sample mapping and is retained for characterisation only. The mapped blanks cover 95 canonical biological profiles for Trip~4 and 217 for Trip~5. These batch memberships are recorded separately from the campaign labels.

PCR negative controls comprised two types. No-template controls (NTCs) contained PCR reagents and nuclease-free water instead of DNA, and reagent-only controls per amplification plate that contained reagents but neither water nor DNA. Reagent-only controls had low or undetectable DNA concentrations and TapeStation profiles of only adapter dimers, so they were not sequenced. We sequenced six NTCs that were prepared with Trip~5 samples.

Positive standards assess recovery of expected genera. The Trips 1 and 2 protocol used the ZymoBIOMICS HMW DNA Standard (D6322), which contained purified DNA and was added at the PCR step. D6322 contains seven bacterial genomes (14\% of genomic DNA each) and one yeast (2\%). The Trip~3 protocol used the ZymoBIOMICS Microbial Community Standard (D6300) which contains whole cells and was therefore included from the extraction step. D6300 contains eight
bacteria (12\% of genomic DNA each) and two yeasts (2\% each). These
proportions are the manufacturer's values. Two of each type of positive standard were amplified and sequenced.

DNA extracted from soil includes DNA from intact cells as well as extracellular DNA and DNA from dead or damaged cells \cite{carini2016relic}. Propidium monoazide (PMA) is a dye that enters only cells with damaged membranes and binds to free DNA, and prevents amplification by PCR when exposed to light. The paired-treatment dataset records 16S rRNA gene profiles from PMA-treated and untreated aliquots of a subset of Trip~5 samples, with shared preparation and exposure conditions.

The PMA dataset includes three soil samples collected from campsites during Trip~5. The source material comprised root-adjacent soil from campsite C1, and root-adjacent and surface soil from campsite C2. From each collection tube, three subsamples were taken and each suspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH~7.4) at a soil-to-buffer ratio of 1:10 (w/v). The three samples from each collection tube are technical replicates of the treatment. Each sample was split into a treated aliquot that included 50~\textmu M PMAxx, and an untreated aliquot, giving nine treated/untreated pairs.

Both aliquots followed the same exposure procedure: they were kept in the dark for 10~min to allow the dye to enter the cells, and then exposed to 450~nm light for 35~min at a distance of 15--20~cm from the light source, and turned every 15~min to ensure even exposure. Aliquots were stored at $-20$\degree C, and DNA was extracted from 860~\textmu L of each aliquot with the PowerSoil Pro kit.


\subsection{Amplicon profiles and predicted functions}
Raw amplicon reads were processed with nf-core/ampliseq (v2.14.0)
\cite{nfcore, ampliseq}, using DADA2 \cite{dada2} for error
correction and denoising, and SILVA (v138.2) \cite{silva} for taxonomic assignment. This resulted in 351,472 amplicon sequence variants (ASVs), and included 1,247 biological samples and 24 control samples. Quality filtering excludes the 24 control profiles and ten biological profiles with fewer than 1,000 reads, including one \texttt{T1Dr1} run with 934 reads. This leaves 1,237 profiles, including 1,227 profiles from sites~1--60, and 1,186 deduplicated specimen profiles for the primary transect; the complete table is distributed unfiltered. The container identity and full denoising parameters are included in the repository.

Gene family and pathway abundances were predicted from the 16S rRNA data with PICRUSt2 v2.4.1 \cite{picrust2}. Two executions used the Trips~1--4 ampliseq output and the Trip~5 output (330,830 ASVs). KEGG Orthology (KO) prediction used the phylogenetic independent contrasts method, edge exponent 0.5, and random seed 100. Enzyme Commission (EC) and KO abundances required 100 reads, and pathway abundances were inferred from the EC predictions.

\subsection{Soil chemistry and climate observations}\label{methods:xrf}
Soil elemental composition was measured by X-ray fluorescence (XRF) using an Olympus Vanta VMR-XXX-G3-E handheld analyser (serial no.\ 841307), both in the field during Trip~5 and in the laboratory on archived soil samples from all campaigns. The field log contains 106 entries across 59 sites. For field measurements, the instrument was used in Geochem (3-Beam) mode with NORMAL settings, and 71 complete measurement sessions from 58 sites were retained, including repeated sessions at nine sites. In the laboratory, we analyzed 725 samples with the instrument in Fast Mode with the Fast Screening-He8mm setting. Instrument-reported elemental and oxide values are stored as separate variables with undocumented units.

To assess agreement between field and laboratory measurements, the comparison paired the median of repeated field sessions at each site with the corresponding Trip~5 laboratory measurements at the 58 sites measured using both methods. This comparison assesses agreement in site-level rankings, with correlations reported in Technical Validation.

The mapping cross-referenced the 93 instrument reporting channels against ChEBI r247 (3 December 2025) and PubChem properties (retrieved 23 July 2026) \cite{chebi,pubchem}. The mapping identified corresponding ChEBI entries for 79 channels and PubChem entries for 91 channels. Verification covered identifier validity, names, chemical formulae, charge, and whether each variable represented an element or an oxide.

\label{methods:ph}
Archived-soil pH measurements cover all five campaigns. Dried soil was sieved to below 2~mm; 1~g was mixed with 2.5~mL of 0.01~M CaCl$_2$. Each suspension was mixed for 30~min in a HulaMixer and left to stand for another 30~min at room temperature before measuring pH.

The pH measurements used an accumet AB315 meter with a Fisher Scientific electrode (cat. no.~13-620-112) calibrated at the start of each session, with the electrode slope and the reading of the pH~10 buffer recorded for every session. The electrode was rinsed with Milli-Q water between measurements. Of 767 recorded measurements, 36 were quarantined because of date provenance and 19 because of quality control. The retained dataset contains 712 measurements with a recorded electrode slope between 95\% and 102\% and a pH~10 buffer reading within 0.1 units of its nominal value (Table~\ref{tab:ph_coverage}). All 156 admitted Trip~4 pH measurements used archived field replicate~2. Each admitted measurement is linked to its physical specimen and retains its original assay identifier. The ecology analysis uses the same measurement values.

\subsection{Shotgun metagenomics and genome catalogue}\label{methods:shotgun}

Shotgun metagenomic sequencing complements the amplicon profiles with
genome assemblies and gene annotations from the same sampling campaigns.
The read-bearing library inventory contains 551 libraries across 61 site
codes (59 primary transect sites, comprising all primary sites except
site~56, plus two Trip~1 named locations at sites~62 and~64): 210 from
Trip~1, 37 from Trip~2, 128 from Trip~3, 85 from Trip~4 and 91 from
Trip~5. Library metadata records the campaign, source site, soil
compartment and replicate. The libraries were sequenced as 554
paired-end runs, 221 on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 and 333 on an Illumina
NovaSeq X; three libraries were sequenced on both instruments. Libraries
for 81 runs were prepared with the NEBNext Ultra II DNA Library Prep Kit
and for 473 runs with the QIAseq FX DNA Library UDI Kit. The shotgun study is registered at ENA
under \texttt{PRJEB125153} (secondary accession \texttt{ERP204894}).

Genome reconstruction combined MEGAHIT v1.2.9 assembly \cite{megahit} with single-sample and
self-supervised SemiBin2 v2.2.1 binning \cite{semibin2}, followed by DAS Tool
v1.1.7 refinement \cite{dastool}. MEGAHIT produced contigs of at least
500~bp for 549 of the 551 libraries; libraries 51Sr1 and F5Dr1 yielded no
contigs. CheckM2 v1.1.0 \cite{checkm2} estimated completeness and contamination for the
refined bins, and 12,434 refined bins reached at least 50\% completeness
with at most 10\% contamination. The bins were dereplicated with dRep \cite{drep}, using
CheckM2 quality estimates, a minimum completeness of 50\%, a maximum
contamination of 10\%, and a minimum genome length of 50,000~bp.
The recorded clustering parameters specify 90\% primary ANI and
95\% secondary ANI, with fastANI for secondary comparisons and a minimum
alignment coverage of 0.1 using dRep's \texttt{larger} coverage mode.
The resulting catalogue contains 5,438 representative identifiers.
The provenance table links each identifier to its source library,
binning strategy and bin, and records quality estimates from the refined-bin
CheckM2 report.

Taxonomic assignments for the refined representatives used GTDB-Tk
v2.7.2 with GTDB release R232 \cite{gtdb}. The two native classification
tables contain 4,847 bacterial and 591 archaeal representatives, and
their genome identifiers match the representative catalogue exactly.
A frozen historical analytical taxonomy snapshot
(\texttt{EQ-TAX-LEGACY-ANALYTICAL-v0}) used by earlier companion
genus/order turnover and proteome scripts shares the same 5,438 genome
identifiers and identical CheckM2 quality metrics, as well as the exact
same 237 Thermoplasmatota genome identifiers, but differs from the
canonical GTDB R232 strings on 1,898 of the 5,438 genomes (zero at
domain, 247 at phylum, 34 at class, 220 at order, 511 at family, 1,124
at genus and 46 at species; 42 differing strings among the 237
Thermoplasmatota genomes, where canonical GTDB R232 assigns four named
orders plus two unassigned and 19 named genera plus 135 unassigned versus
four named orders plus zero unassigned and 14 named genera plus 139
unassigned in the frozen snapshot). The two taxonomy snapshots are
linked 1:1 in the packaged reconciliation table and never conflated.
The annotation workflow detected rRNA genes with barrnap v0.9 and tRNA genes with tRNAscan-SE
v2.0.12 \cite{trnascan}, and assigned each representative a quality tier
following the MIMAG standard \cite{mimag}. Of the 5,438 representatives,
126 meet the high-quality criteria, which require more than 90\%
completeness, less than 5\% contamination, the 23S, 16S and 5S rRNA genes
and tRNAs for at least 18 of the 20 amino acids. Another 5,311 meet the
strict medium-quality criteria of at least 50\% completeness and less than 10\%
contamination, giving 5,437 strict HQ+MQ representatives deposited as ENA
MAGs. The remaining representative, \texttt{48Dr2\_\_self\_SemiBin\_25}
(completeness 94.22\%, contamination 10.0\%), sits at the 10\% contamination
boundary; it is classified as below-medium under strict MIMAG criteria and
deposited in ENA as a binned metagenome, while operational dRep analyses
using the inclusive $\le 10\%$ contamination threshold include all 5,438
species representatives (126 high-quality and 5,312 non-high-quality
representatives).

Two distinct gene-calling products are maintained across the 5,438
representatives (Table~\ref{tab:products}). In the canonical release
annotation product (snapshot identifier \texttt{EQ-PROT-BAKTA-v1.0.0}),
the genes of each representative were annotated with Bakta v1.12.0
\cite{bakta} and its full database v6.0, predicting coding sequences and
translated proteins alongside rRNA and tRNA genes. eggNOG-mapper v2.1.13
\cite{eggnog} searched these predicted proteins against the eggNOG v5.0.2
database with DIAMOND v2.0.15 to assign functional annotations, KEGG
orthologs (KOs), Pfam domains and CAZy families (counts in
Table~\ref{tab:products}). Per-genome summaries count the predicted
proteins, the proteins with an eggNOG assignment and the proteins with at
least one KO, and list the genome--feature pairs that occur in the
annotations. In the companion analysis protein product (snapshot
identifier \texttt{EQ-PROT-PYRODIGAL-REP-v1.0.0}), Pyrodigal predicted
20,451,986 proteins associated with the same 5,438 representative genome
identifiers (reconciled against 20,379,930 bin-mapped assembly protein
calls drawn from 753,305,533 whole-assembly protein predictions),
supplying the sequence universe for downstream functional and archaeal
proteome analyses \cite{ecology_companion,proteome_companion}. Direct
locus-by-locus equivalence between the 19,186,792 Bakta predictions and
the 20,451,986 Pyrodigal predictions has not been established; matching
genome identifiers do not establish identical underlying FASTA sequence
contents or retained contig coordinates, and the two protein sets are
maintained as separate products. Companion genome-resolved KO projections
likewise use a frozen analytical KO matrix
(\texttt{EQ-KO-LEGACY-ANALYTICAL-v0}, 11,395 distinct KOs across the
5,438 representatives and 8,860 KOs shared with PICRUSt2) that is
recorded separately from \texttt{EQ-PROT-BAKTA-v1.0.0}.

Four read-mapping products with distinct snapshot identifiers record
representative abundance and detection across shotgun libraries. First, to
estimate per-library coverage in the canonical depth/breadth product
(\texttt{EQ-COVERM-REFINED-v1.0.0}), we mapped the reads of all 551
libraries to a single reference that contains every contig of the 5,438
representatives. Each contig identifier was prefixed with its genome
identifier, so that contigs with the same assembler-assigned name in
different genomes remain distinct. Bowtie2 v2.5.1 \cite{bowtie2} aligned
the first 200 million read pairs of each library in
\texttt{--very-sensitive} mode and retained aligned reads only; SAMtools
v1.16.1 \cite{samtools} sorted the alignments. CoverM v0.6.1
\cite{coverm} in genome mode counted reads aligned with at least 95\%
identity and reported, for every genome, the mean depth, the covered
fraction, the trimmed mean depth, the number of mapped reads and the
genome length. Second, a CoverM v0.7.0 genome-mode relative-abundance
matrix (\texttt{EQ-COVERM-REL-ABUND-v1.0.0}, retaining the unmapped read
fraction across 573 matrix columns including 551 study libraries) defines
the current genome-resolved ecological cohorts \cite{ecology_companion}.
Exact specimen joins and post-QC read-depth availability define these
nested subsets (Table~\ref{tab:shotgun_cohorts}), while per-library
admission flags and excluded sample identifiers are recorded in the
packaged cohort accounting table. Third, an
archaeal competitive-mapping product
(\texttt{EQ-COVERM-ARCHAEA-COMP-v1.0.0}) maps 193 paired metagenomic
profiles across 68 site-campaign visits and 36 sites against the 5,438
catalogue representatives combined with 301 external Thermoplasmatota
reference genomes across 0\%, 10\%, 20\% and 50\% genome-breadth gates,
with a \texttt{MAPQ} $\ge 20$ filter evaluated as a separate sensitivity
analysis \cite{proteome_companion}. Fourth, a historical Trips~1--3 pilot
dataset (\texttt{EQ-COVERM-LEGACY-991-v0}) retains 150 CoverM profiles
(58, 15 and 77 libraries from Trips~1--3) mapped against an earlier
991-genome draft catalogue, eggNOG-mapper v2.1.12 \cite{eggnog}
annotations yielding a 990-genome KO copy-number matrix, and six source
tables for an initial MAG-derived versus PICRUSt2 comparison; this pilot
product is preserved for audit reproducibility and superseded for
full-transect analyses by the 5,438-representative products.

\subsection{Genome-resolved trait annotation}
\label{methods:microtrait}

We profiled all 5,438 representative genomes with microTrait \cite{karaoz2022microtrait}, using their checksum-verified nucleotide sequences. This product, \texttt{EQ-MICROTRAIT-\allowbreak REP-\allowbreak v1.0.0}, uses fresh Prodigal v2.6.3 calls in metagenomic mode and retains its own gene identifiers and provenance, separately from the Bakta and companion Pyrodigal protein sets. Profile searches used the pinned microTrait models and the selected dbCAN V14 glycoside-hydrolase models, with the published profile thresholds and logical trait rules. The package records the exact microTrait and gRodon source revisions, database assets, software environment and execution parameters.

The outputs comprise model-hit and rule matrices, trait matrices at three levels of aggregation, and per-genome growth and optimum-temperature predictions with applicability flags. Trait values count satisfied rules within a category, with binary categories capped at one. Predicted growth and temperature values describe model-derived genomic properties; their interpretation retains genome completeness, fragmentation and archaeal applicability information. Of 1,831 model identifiers required by the rules, 1,829 were assayed. The release lacked the \texttt{aphA} and \texttt{phoN} models. For the phosphatase rule combining these models with \texttt{phoA} and \texttt{phoD}, the interpretation-safe table records a positive call when either assayed model supports it and otherwise records an unassessed value. The unmodified software output is retained alongside this coverage-aware table.

\subsection{Reproducible workflow}
A Nextflow \cite{di2017nextflow} workflow generates the knowledge graph modules, runs the validation checks, and computes the statistics reported in this manuscript. Configuration profiles are available for running the workflow locally or on a computing cluster. Sample processing (Section~\ref{methods}) was run separately with its own parameters and software environments.

The workflow produces each versioned release of the knowledge graph by generating the data modules, comparing their checksums with those listed in the release manifest, loading them into a new database, and checking the results of test queries against the source data. The public service is switched to the new release only after these checks pass. The release manifests list the fixed input files and the generated modules along with their checksums.
