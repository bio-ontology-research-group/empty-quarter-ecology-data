\section{Methods}\label{methods}

\subsection{Study area and sampling design}

We conducted five sampling expeditions across the Rub' al-Khali along
a transect from the western escarpment (19.15\degree N, 45.17\degree
E) to the eastern border of Saudi Arabia with Oman (20.71\degree N,
54.73\degree E). The 60 ordered sampling sites yield a 1,043-km
cumulative path and lie 1,015~km apart end to end. Figure~\ref{fig:transect}
illustrates the altitude profile across this transect. The release workflow
regenerates this figure and its cumulative-distance profile directly from the
60-site latitude, longitude and altitude table and requires the image bytes to
match the staged manuscript figure.

The planned repeated transect encompasses 60 primary sampling sites
at predetermined geographic coordinates. The site catalogue also
contains 10 named locations of special interest (including water
wells, a hot spring and an oil-spill site). The four Trip~1-only
numeric identifiers described below are aliases of four named catalogue
records and are therefore not added to the catalogue count.
The site generator assigns sites 1--60 directly from their numeric
labels and assigns the 10 named locations from the versioned bijection
in \texttt{metadata/samples/site\_iri\_registry.tsv}. It sorts source
sheets and numbers same-label locations from west to east, and fails if
the observed named-site set differs from the registry; site identity
therefore does not depend on filesystem iteration order.

To capture temporal variability, we conducted expeditions in late
winter 2023 (Trip~1), summer 2023 (Trip~2, truncated after site~8
because of extreme temperatures), winter 2024 (Trip~3), summer 2024
(Trip~4), and autumn 2025 (Trip~5). Table~\ref{tab:env_summary}
describes the records in the field environmental logs rather than
implying that every differently labelled record is a distinct physical
site. The Trip~3 source sheet contains 60 primary-site records and five
named special-interest records from the February 2024 expedition. It
also contains 15 appended auxiliary or revisit records whose original
workbook dates are 17--20 March 2023; we consequently assign those
records to Trip~1 rather than extending Trip~3 into March 2024.
Available temperature, atmospheric-pressure and relative-humidity
measurements differ by expedition. We acquired X-ray fluorescence
(XRF) data through two
separate workflows: handheld measurements in the field during Trip~5
and laboratory measurements of archived material from all five
expeditions. These workflows differ in setting, specimen coverage and
analytical role and are therefore not treated as interchangeable
replicates.

The numeric identifiers 61--64 in the Trip~1 source sheets denote
genuine Trip-1-only sampling sites, not malformed records. Their
regions were inaccessible on subsequent expeditions. We retain their
36 source rows in release accounting and their available observations
in descriptive summaries, but exclude them from inference over the
intended repeated-campaign frame of sites 1--60. Their recorded
coordinates identify, in order, the existing catalogue individuals
\emph{Site water well (location 2)}, \emph{Site road (location 1)},
\emph{Site road (location 2)} and \emph{Site camground}. A versioned
alias ledger preserves each numeric source identifier, coordinate,
canonical label and canonical IRI. The specimen generator uses that
ledger to link the 36 rows to the existing site individuals; it does
not mint duplicate site individuals or change their Trip-1-only
campaign role. Coordinate matching also resolves the corresponding
named Trip~1 field-log record for each of the four sites, so every
generated sampling process is linked to the appropriate Trip~1 visit.

The supplementary environmental table is generated from the immutable
field sheets through an exact-cell correction ledger. In the Trip~2
sheet, the eight values 34.5--41.9 occur in the source humidity column
while the temperature column is empty; their sequence and the field
note that the trip ended because of extreme temperature support the
pre-existing RDF-generator interpretation of these values as
temperature. We therefore move those values to temperature and do not
infer Trip~2 pressure or humidity. The Trip~3 site~21 humidity of
31.321\% is retained because the original workbook records the
fraction 0.31321. The same workbook supplies the March 2023 dates for
the 15 appended auxiliary/revisit records; the ledger restores those
dates after a later commit changed only the year to 2024 without source
evidence. By contrast, the Trip~5 site~40 source humidity of 194\% is
outside the physical range. Until a primary source establishes a
correction, the curated value remains missing and the raw value is
preserved only in the correction ledger. The generator fails if a
source cell no longer matches its ledger entry or if any retained
measurement lies outside its declared physical range.
The curated table retains all 274 nonblank records. The graph generator
links 259 primary records and the auxiliary record with the exact
catalogue label \texttt{19.5}, yielding 260 site visits with 260
temperature, 187 pressure and 251 humidity values. The other 14
auxiliary/revisit labels denote offset or special-purpose locations and
remain table-only; coincident coordinates are not treated as evidence
that they are identical to a catalogue site.

At each site, we collected samples from three distinct soil
compartments: bulk surface soil (0 cm depth), bulk subsurface soil
(5--10 cm depth, hereafter ``deep''), and subsurface soil collected
near roots (5--10 cm depth, hereafter ``root-adjacent''). The companion
ecology analysis calls the same 5--10 cm bulk compartment ``shallow
subsurface''; in this resource and its graph, the legacy label
``deep'' and the class label ``Deep Soil Sample'' denote that same
source compartment and do not imply a greater sampling depth. We
obtained three co-located field replicates per compartment by placing soil
into sterile tubes, either by scooping soil directly into the tube or
using sterile plastic spoons. We preserved samples at $-20$\degree C
in the field using an Engel MR040F portable freezer. At the end of each
expedition, we transferred samples to an $-80$\degree C freezer in the
laboratory.
These replicates are not independent sites. Companion ecological
analyses aggregate or block them by site, campaign and position before
site-level inference.

Most canonical bacterial-community profile identifiers encode the
expedition, numeric site, compartment and field replicate. Trip~1 has
no prefix; Trips~2--5 use \texttt{T}, \texttt{F}, \texttt{S} and
\texttt{V}, respectively. Compartment codes are \texttt{S} (surface),
\texttt{D} (5--10~cm bulk soil) and \texttt{PR} (root-adjacent soil),
followed by \texttt{r1}--\texttt{r3}. Trip~5 suffixes
\texttt{O}, \texttt{T}, \texttt{R} and \texttt{RE} denote extraction,
library-preparation or re-extraction events rather than additional
field replicates. The release ledger preserves exceptions and links
profiles to source records instead of relying on string parsing alone.

The source ledger identifies 34 master-sheet control rows and
distinguishes controls, failed extractions, reruns and exclusions by
expedition and source row; a further 26 control-labelled rows occur in
the sequence submissions. Author-confirmed control facts are frozen in
\path{evidence/controls/control_ground_truth.tsv}. The numbered positive
and negative controls are replicate groups, but a reused label is not
treated as a globally unique material identifier.

The positive-control design differed by expedition. Trips~1 and 2 used
the ZymoBIOMICS HMW DNA Standard D6322, supplied as purified
high-molecular-weight DNA and therefore entering after extraction.
Trip~3 used the whole-cell ZymoBIOMICS Microbial Community Standard
D6300 and carried it through extraction. Trip~4 had no positive
control. Trip~5 used D6300, but its positive and negative control pair
was sequenced by shotgun metagenomics rather than by the 16S assay.
D6322 contains seven bacterial genomes at 14\,\% genomic DNA each and
\emph{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} at 2\,\%; D6300 contains eight bacterial
genomes at 12\,\% each and two yeasts at 2\,\% each. These
manufacturer genomic-DNA fractions are composition specifications, not
expected 16S read fractions.

Negative controls included distinct extraction blanks and PCR blanks.
\texttt{EB1}--\texttt{EB18} and
\texttt{Negative1}, \texttt{Negative2}, and
\texttt{Negative4}--\texttt{Negative7} are confirmed extraction-blank
labels. One EB was included per extraction day, and an extraction day
could contain samples from several expeditions; an EB is therefore
linked to its extraction batch, never directly to a trip. The
extraction-day workbook maps EB1--EB17 to dates and 220 Trip~5
biological profiles; these links do not assign an EB exclusively to an
expedition. EB18 and the six \texttt{Negative}-labelled libraries remain
unmapped to an extraction day. The Trip~4 workbook records five
numbered blanks and one unnumbered blank, but the blank covering a
three-day extraction was not sequenced, leaving all or part of 23 sites
without a sequenced blank. Sequence indices distinguish library
occurrences but do not establish upstream material identity. The
metadata-negative profile \texttt{e0323\_Ctrl\_1\_Trip1} is consequently
not reclassified from its ambiguous name. No sterile-bag inventory was
recovered, so no field-blank instance is invented.

The control registry already assigns PCR-blank roles to three sequenced 16S
libraries with explicit PCR or NTC labels
(\texttt{e0555\_PCR\_Ctrl\_Trip1}, \texttt{e0875\_NTC\_2} and
\texttt{e8667\_PCRCtrl}) and to one Trip~4 workbook record. The workbook record
lists index 275 but has no matched FASTQ dataset. The remaining PCR-control metadata
task is laboratory confirmation of any additional reused or generic labels,
their PCR batches and the biological libraries amplified in the same batches.
Until that evidence is frozen, unresolved label-to-batch claims remain
dispositions rather than ground-truth graph edges.

\subsection{DNA extraction and sequencing}

We utilized 0.9--1.2 g of soil for DNA extraction using the PowerLyzer
PowerSoil Kit (QIAGEN, catalog \#12855-50) or the DNeasy PowerSoil Pro
Kit (QIAGEN, catalog \#47014). We homogenized all samples with the
PowerLyzer 24 Homogenizer and completed extraction either manually or
via the automated QIACube Connect system. We quantified the eluted DNA
using the Qubit HS dsDNA kit.

We amplified bacterial DNA using the Bakt\_341F
(\texttt{CCTACGGGNGGCWGCAG}) and Bakt\_785R
(\texttt{GACTACHVGGGTATCTAATCC}) primer pair
\cite{klindworth2013primers} to target the V3--V4 region of the 16S
rRNA gene. Both primers carried Illumina overhang adapter sequences.
We performed amplification using the Multiplex PCR kit
(QIAGEN, catalog 206143) with an initial activation at 95\,\textdegree C
for 15\,min, 35 cycles of 94\,\textdegree C for 30\,s,
57\,\textdegree C for 90\,s and 72\,\textdegree C for 90\,s, and a
final extension at 72\,\textdegree C for 10\,min, and sequenced the
resulting pools on an Illumina NovaSeq~6000 platform.
We processed all samples in the PI's laboratory at KAUST.

The reverse primer identity is a correction to the previously recorded
protocol, which named the Apprill 806RB primer
(\texttt{GGACTACNVGGGTWTCTAAT}). A bounded audit of the raw reads
resolves the discrepancy against the recorded value: streaming the
first 10,000 reads of three Trips~1--4 and three Trip~5 biological
read pairs, 56,891 of 60,000 forward reads (94.82\%) begin with
Bakt\_341F and 55,057 of 60,000 reverse reads (91.76\%) begin with
Bakt\_785R, whereas 11 reverse reads (0.018\%) match the 806RB
sequence. The staged protocol record
\path{metadata/protocols/lib_prep/16S_amplicon_lib_prep.md} carries the
corrected pair together with the superseded entry, so the change is
auditable rather than silent.

\subsection{Sequencing data processing}

We processed raw amplicon reads with nf-core/ampliseq (v2.14.0)
\cite{nfcore, ampliseq}, applying the DADA2 algorithm for error
correction and denoising and assigning taxonomy against the SILVA 138.2
reference database \cite{silva}. The exact command, including the
Bakt\_341F and Bakt\_785R primer sequences, the
\texttt{--illumina\_novaseq} model and the resource configuration, is
archived in \path{metadata/amplicon/}. That directory also contains a
portable samplesheet regenerated from the deposited submission records
by \path{scripts/amplicon/build_portable_samplesheet.py}: 1,268
submitted run rows covering 1,238 distinct sample identifiers, of
which 30 carry a second library. Read paths are expressed relative to a
single environment variable rather than the absolute cluster paths of
the original sheet. No container digest was recorded for the run, so
the exact image versions are not pinned.

The canonical Trips~1--5 feature table is the direct output of that
pipeline. It is unfiltered: it retains all 351,472 ASVs and still
contains the 24 control profiles. No contaminant removal has been
applied to it, and none is claimed. A QIIME2 \cite{qiime2}
\emph{decontam} branch exists under \path{analysis/scripts/}, but it
operated on a different and smaller table (531 and 975 sample columns),
and in the form that survives, the command that would have produced the
contaminant table is commented out while a later step consumes that
missing artifact. No removal fraction from that branch is therefore
defensible, and the previously reported prevalence threshold is not
restated as an applied filter. That branch retained ASVs with a minimum
total frequency of five reads in at least two samples.

An assay-aware audit following low-biomass control recommendations
\cite{fierer2025controls} is instead generated by
\path{scripts/controls/run_assay_aware_control_audit.py}. The primary
test pools the 17 extraction-day blanks with frozen links to Trip~5 profiles
and uses an uncorrected one-sided Fisher $p<0.1$, requiring presence in
at least two blanks. At the same threshold, requiring only one blank
would call 12,051 rather than 351 ASVs. Because one blank represents each
extraction batch, within-batch prevalence estimation is impossible.
The resulting candidate set is applied only to the 217 canonical
biological profiles linked to those batches; EB18 and the six
\texttt{Negative}-labelled libraries are characterization-only.
Frequency modelling is not run because control DNA concentrations are
unavailable. Seven unambiguously positive-labelled 16S profiles from
Trips~1--3 in the frozen pre-decontamination table are used to evaluate
expected bacterial-genus recovery and exact-ASV occurrence, never to
train the contaminant classifier. Their library-to-product assignments
are provisional hypotheses from positive labels and the confirmed
trip-level design. Expected bacterial genera accounted for 5.85, 12.33,
31.05, 0, 0.05, 100 and 99.99\,\% of reads in
\texttt{e0553\_Ctrl\_2}, \texttt{e0554\_Ctrl\_3},
\texttt{e0872\_TMC1}, \texttt{e0874\_Positive},
\texttt{e8294\_FPosCtrl1}, \texttt{e8661\_FPosCtrl2} and
\texttt{e8665\_FPosCtrl3}, respectively. Only the final two met the
declared descriptive mock-dominated threshold of at least 95\,\%.
Manufacturer genomic-DNA percentages are not expected 16S read
percentages. The primary table remains unfiltered;
\path{scripts/controls/run_control_ecology_sensitivity.sh} rebuilds the
ecological inputs and 25 tracked headline metrics across 11 claims
solely as a bounded sensitivity analysis (Section~\ref{usage}).

Genome-encoded functional potential used for the companion analysis was
predicted with PICRUSt2 v2.4.1; its execution manifest, with the exact
commands, the PIC hidden-state method with edge exponent 0.5 and seed
100, the NSTI cut-off of 2.0, the minimum-read threshold of 100 and the
recorded resources, is archived at \path{metadata/functional/PICRUST2.md}.
The predictions came from two separately executed input sets: the
Trips~1--4 ampliseq output and the corrected Trip~5 ampliseq output, whose
input universe contained 330,830 ASVs. They were not generated from the
later 351,472-ASV combined canonical table.

All deterministic metadata corrections are recorded without
overwriting the source values. In records \texttt{F46Dr2},
\texttt{S46Dr2} and \texttt{V46Dr2}, the value \texttt{NEBNext} had
shifted into the site column while the other populated cells in those
rows remained aligned. The confirmed correction ledger restores site
46, records \texttt{NEBNext} as the displaced WGS library-preparation
kit value and retains the original cell value and rationale. The repair
does not infer that WGS was performed: sequencing status and run
availability remain separate fields and are unchanged.

\subsection{Companion-analysis derived inputs}

The staged release candidate contains the exact derived inputs needed
to re-execute the reported downstream companion analyses. The
\path{metadata/relic-dna/PMA_ASV_table.tsv} matrix has 18 analytical
columns representing nine Trip~5 treated--untreated aliquot pairs,
with controls retained separately. The
\path{metadata/metagenome/coverm_profiles.tar.gz} archive contains 150
sample-level genome-abundance profiles, and
\path{metadata/metagenome/eq.emapper.annotations.gz} supplies the
eggNOG annotations used to construct the encoded-function matrix for
the 990 genomes shared with those profiles. The
\path{metadata/metagenome/measured_function_inputs.tar.gz} archive
contains the six exact source tables used for the genome-derived versus
PICRUSt2 comparison and metabolic-marker summary.

These are derived statistical inputs, not a replacement for the
underlying sequence data. The PMA table and measured-function archive
directly support their named endpoints; the encoded-function null also
requires the bulk-listed eggNOG file. These inputs do not reproduce
upstream shotgun or PMA read processing, metagenome assembly, binning, dereplication, annotation
or CoverM generation. Raw shotgun and PMA
reads and the full
metagenome-assembled-genome catalogue therefore remain separate
deposition requirements.

\subsection{XRF acquisition, processing and reconciliation}\label{methods:xrf}

\paragraph{Field-XRF.}
During Trip~5, researchers used a Vanta handheld instrument to perform
measurements in the field. The source log contains 106 entries across
59 sites; 71 complete sessions at 58 sites were retained, and nine of
those sites have repeated complete sessions. The field table retains
the instrument test identifier, site identifier, reported analyte
values and instrument uncertainty columns. Project records identify
these as deep-soil measurements, but the instrument log itself does
not encode a physical-sample, compartment or depth identifier.
Field/laboratory linkage is consequently site-level, not a
same-aliquot comparison. The exports encode the
\texttt{Geochem(3-Beam)} method and \texttt{NORMAL} mode.

\paragraph{Laboratory-XRF.}
Archived material was subsequently measured in the laboratory for all
five expeditions. The canonical inputs contain 547 selected sample
records from Trips~1--4 and 178 selected records from Trip~5
(725 records in total), and all 725 are used in the analyses. A former
158-row Trip~5 table and the resulting 705-row cross-trip table are
retired provenance artifacts; neither is a current analytical input.
Trip~5 workbooks
encode the \texttt{Fast Screening-He8mm} method, \texttt{He} mode,
8-mm diameter and oxide material. Equivalent acquisition metadata
have not been recovered for Trips~1--4.

\paragraph{Aggregation audit.}
The provenance audit reproduced all 13,390 reported Trips~1--4 cells
under the current largest-positive-value rule and all 4,293 reported
Trip~5 cells under the parser's different last-reported-value rule.
Fifteen rare analytes present in the Trips~1--4 source rows are absent
from the current wide-table schema. Neither rule is yet treated as
instrument-prescribed: a larger value is not necessarily more
accurate, and zero means only ``not reported positive'' while
detection-limit semantics remain unavailable. The audit compares
maximum-positive, mean-positive, median-positive, first-reported,
last-reported and workbook-section-specific candidates, but selecting
these source-specific policies does not establish instrument accuracy.
The release preserves and discloses the Trips~1--4 maximum-positive
and Trip~5 last-reported policies instead of implying that they are a
common calibrated procedure. Alternative summaries are retained as a
sensitivity audit; they are not substituted into the primary table.

\paragraph{Chemical-identifier audit.}
All instrument channels are declared once in
\path{config/codes/xrf_chemical_mapping.yml}. Element channels
were mapped to neutral-atom records; oxide channels were mapped only
when the pinned source contained a neutral formula-matching record.
The executable audit streams the configured identifiers from pinned
ChEBI v247, rejects missing or deprecated records and mismatches in
formula, charge or atom-versus-oxide type, and performs the same
checks against a dated PubChem property snapshot. ChEBI formulae that
use dot notation are compositionally parsed. Two documented cases are
handled explicitly: ChEBI does not assert formula or charge metadata
for its exact-label magnesium-oxide record, and P$_4$O$_{10}$ is
accepted for the P$_2$O$_5$ reporting channel only as an empirical
oxide-equivalent. No presence of a particular oxide molecule or
mineral is inferred. Unsupported mappings remain null; LE remains an
unmapped instrument pseudo-analyte.

For a descriptive cross-workflow diagnostic, repeated field sessions
were reduced by their site median and matched to the Trip~5 laboratory
deep-soil record at 58 sites. This is not same-aliquot validation.
Across the reported analytes, rank agreement was generally modest and
field/laboratory ratios were analyte-dependent. The canonical
analytical table and regenerated knowledge graph use all 725 laboratory records
while keeping the field sessions as a separate observation type. No
cross-workflow calibration is inferred, and no ecological
interpretation assumes that field and laboratory measurements have the
same scale or are interchangeable.

\subsection{Archived-soil pH acquisition and admission}\label{methods:ph}

We measured archived dry soil after sieving below 2~mm and mixing without
fine grinding. Each assay used an independent 1~g scoop suspended in 2.5~mL
0.01~M CaCl$_2$. Measurements were made with an accumet AB315 and validated
Probe~3 under automatic temperature compensation. Each measurement session
recorded a two-point pH 7/10 calibration, electrode slope and pH-10 buffer
read-back; admission required calibration marked yes, slope 95--102\,\% and a
read-back within 0.1 pH units of nominal. Historical probe-comparison and
reference-soil sheets document method validation but are not biological
observations.

All measurements available when laboratory follow-up ended on 3 August 2026
were frozen as \texttt{EQ-PH-SHARED-v1.0.0}. This is the sole pH version used
by this descriptor and the companion ecology paper. The source workbook and
version manifest are under
\path{metadata/samples/ph/versions/EQ-PH-SHARED-v1.0.0/}; the normalized audit
tables are under \path{metadata/ph/}. We reconciled each trip-sheet row to the
sample ledger and existing specimen IRI. A row entered the graph only when it
also carried a numeric pH value between 0 and 14 and a source date represented
as an Excel date, ISO date or day-first \texttt{DD/MM/YYYY} date that did not
fall after the cutoff. Unparseable dates and dates that resolved beyond the
cutoff were quarantined. We neither inferred missing quality-control fields
nor imputed unavailable values.

The audit accounts for all 1,168 source rows. It admits 712 measurements,
retains 356 rows without a measurement, marks 45 as
depleted, quarantines 36 with ambiguous dates, and excludes 19 numeric rows
whose recorded quality-control fields are incomplete. The measurement campaign
is therefore complete as available, not complete for every listed specimen.
For Trip~4, \texttt{S28Sr1} was missed during preparation and
\texttt{S57Dr1} was depleted. Future corrections or recovered measurements
require a new version; they cannot replace this frozen source.

The workbook has no session identifier. We reconstructed 29 session
individuals from unique combinations of trip, accepted measurement date,
electrode slope and pH-10 read-back. These groupings preserve the available
quality-control context but are not directly recorded laboratory batch IDs.

\subsection{Environmental and climate data}

We retrieved historical climate data from the Open-Meteo archive
endpoint \cite{openmeteo} for each recorded field location in two
separately configured acquisitions. The monthly acquisition covered
1~January 2022 to 20~January 2026 and requested daily mean temperature,
total precipitation, rainfall and mean relative humidity before
aggregation to monthly means and sums. A separate daily table covered
1~January 2022 to 1~February 2026 and requested temperature, total
precipitation and rainfall, but not humidity. The regenerated graph
contains 12,936 monthly climate measurement records encompassing
temperature, total precipitation, rainfall and relative humidity. The annual source fields
contain 636 values for temperature, total precipitation and rainfall;
exact site-key resolution retains 582 values in the graph and
quarantines 54 values attached to unresolved field-notebook labels.

Retrieval keyed on the \texttt{Site} column of the per-trip geodata
sheets, which is a field notebook column rather than an integer primary
key. The site catalogue contains 70 sampling-site individuals: 60
numbered sites and ten named locations. Applying the same label
resolution as the RDF generator, we indexed each individual by its
\texttt{rdfs:label} and, for labels beginning \emph{Site}, by the
remainder. Under this rule, 66 of the 81 distinct fetch keys resolve to a site
individual: all 60 numbered sites and six named ones addressed by their
bare label (\emph{oill spill}, \emph{hot spring}, \emph{Camel1},
\emph{plant gradient}, \texttt{19.5} and \emph{camground}). That is the
denominator behind the released counts: $66 \times 49$ months $\times$
four variables gives 12,936 monthly records.

The remaining 15 keys resolve to no site individual. Thirteen carry a
numeric stem with an unrecorded offset or suffix (\texttt{27+},
\texttt{47+grass}, \texttt{52AD} and similar) and two are free-text
locations (\emph{road}, \emph{water well}) that do not match a
catalogue label. A near-site label is deliberately not mapped onto its
stem: \texttt{47+grass} records a reading taken near site~47, not a
reading of site~47, and the offset was never written down.
\path{scripts/climate/curate_climate_keys.py} resolves every key
against \path{ontology/rubalkhali_sites.owl}, records the disposition
and resolved site IRI in \path{climate_fetch_key_ledger.tsv}, and
writes site-resolved projections of the monthly (3,234 rows retained,
735 quarantined) and daily (98,538 retained, 22,395 quarantined)
tables. The same exact-key rule retains 194 of 212 geodata rows with
annual fields (582 of 636 values) and quarantines the remaining 18 rows
(54 values); annual records are not coordinate-matched onto a
catalogue site.

Acquisition provenance is only partly recoverable. The endpoint,
request parameters, date window and input and output checksums are
recorded in \path{climate_acquisition_frozen.json}. The raw response
bodies, the per-request timestamps and any upstream model or dataset
version were never captured for the archived tables, so the historic
acquisition cannot be reproduced byte-for-byte and is not claimed to
be. The acquisition code has since been given an explicit frozen date
window and writes response snapshots, which makes a *future* retrieval
re-derivable from its own snapshots; it does not retrospectively
reconstruct the archived one, and the first such retrieval still has to
contact the service.
The citations to MIxS/MIMARKS and STREAMS provide reporting context;
this package does not claim audited compliance with either standard. A
field-level crosswalk and completed reporting checklist remain
pre-submission author tasks.
Furthermore, field researchers
manually classified each site according to its biome (e.g., desert
biome) and specific environmental features (e.g., sand dune, saline
pan, gravel, desert oasis). We subsequently mapped these
classifications to the Environment Ontology (ENVO) \cite{envo}.

\subsection{Schema validation}

To ensure structural integrity within the RDF graph, we used Shape
Expressions (ShEx) \cite{shex}. ShEx provides a formal structural
schema language that validates the presence, cardinality, and datatype
of RDF properties. We defined specific ShEx shapes for all core entity
types in the knowledge graph. These entity types include sampling
sites, soil samples, DNA extracts, sequencing datasets, measurements,
quality control records, laboratory control materials, control roles,
laboratory batches, control sequence occurrences, archived-soil pH processes,
values and sessions, and taxonomy mappings.

The validation workflow binds node selectors (for example, all
instances of the sampling-site class) to their ShEx definitions using
a Shape Map and fails if it detects a missing mandatory property (for
example, site coordinates) or an invalid datatype. The site, specimen,
environmental-measurement, DNA, XRF, sequence-record and QC modules
passed their assigned shapes. The separate control module also passed
its shape and scientific-invariant checks, including unique role
bearers, paired input/output and role-realization directions, no direct
control-to-trip or batch-to-trip link, and no positive control in the
contaminant-training set. The multi-gigabyte taxonomy ABox was
excluded from this memory-bounded ShEx run; the exact generated file
was instead parsed in full with an independent Turtle parser and
checked by a streaming validator over every classification
process--dataset--quality--value relationship and identifier range.
Passing establishes conformance to the declared structural contract
appropriate to each stated validator; it does not establish source
accuracy or biological validity, and the streaming check is not
reported as an unexecuted ShEx result.
The pH module was validated separately over all 712
process--quality--value sets and 29 sessions. Its positive Shape Map passed,
while a single-defect fixture lacking UO:0000196 was rejected as expected. The
Turtle and RDF/XML serializations were also required to be graph-equivalent.
The validator uses the Apache Jena ShEx library (version 4.10.0)
invoked from custom Groovy scripts.

\subsection{Reasoning coverage}

We use ELK \cite{elk} for a bounded consistency test over the loaded
modules and the OWL~2~EL constructs it supports. ELK is not complete
for arbitrary OWL~2~DL constructs, including universal restrictions
and inverse properties used by imported SIO modules. Consequently, a
successful ELK run establishes neither full OWL~2~DL consistency nor
the absence of every possible ABox contradiction. In the current
memory-bounded LITE run, ELK reported the merged included modules as
consistent. The run did not precompute the class hierarchy and
therefore did not test whether uninstantiated named classes were
unsatisfiable. Large reference and taxonomy modules were excluded, so
this result is bounded consistency of the included modules rather than
whole-graph logical consistency.

\subsection{Reproducible workflow}

Release generation, validation and manuscript-derived statistics are
orchestrated from one Nextflow entry point (\texttt{main.nf}).
Separate local/workstation and IBEX Slurm profiles change execution
infrastructure without changing process definitions. The executed
workstation run generated the source-provenance snapshot, sample-flow
ledger, release reconciliation, XRF audit, ecology analyses,
resolution-matched functional null and network analysis. In an
isolated task it also regenerated the site, environmental-measurement,
sample, XRF, DNA, sequence-record and QC modules from their declared
sources and required every byte stream to match the staged release
candidate. The same generated-module stage rebuilds the shared pH module from
its frozen workbook, admission policy and specimen graph. The separate
\path{scripts/analysis/run_ph_shared_v1.sh} driver reproduces the pH ecology
models, version comparison, manuscript values and full ShEx validation. The
corrected taxonomy mapping and full taxonomy ABox were
then generated using that fresh sequence-record module, and the
knowledge-graph gates consumed those exact upstream artifacts rather
than the checkout's prebuilt ABoxes. The curated base TBox/RBox is a
versioned workflow input, not a data-derived output. The paper build is
a separate leaf stage so that rendering can run on a TeX-enabled host
without changing any scientific result.
