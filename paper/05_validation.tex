\section{Technical validation}\label{valid}

Validation assesses source-to-release reconciliation, assay controls, sequence-file integrity and the accuracy of represented links. Structural constraints and logical checks assess the graph within their stated coverage. Expected query results provide an independent comparison with source observations. For every check, the validation records identify input files by checksum and the software versions used (Section~\ref{records}).

\subsection{Source reconciliation and measurement validation}

The archived evidence workflow produced a specimen catalogue and
reconciliation summaries for specimens, extracts, libraries, runs and
community profiles. The 2,516 eligible source rows reconcile exactly
to 2,516 generated sample individuals; the 1,647 extraction-eligible
rows reconcile to 1,647 DNA extracts; and 1,242 resolved sequence rows
reconcile to 1,242 amplicon libraries and 1,242 FASTQ datasets. A
site-identity check independently matches each Trip~1 numeric identifier
61--64 to a named catalogue individual by exact coordinates and
rejects coordinate or IRI disagreement, accounting for all 36 corresponding
source specimens.

Environmental validation accounts for 274 field observations and checks
site identity, temperature, pressure and relative humidity. Values enter
the curated environmental dataset only when they satisfy the measurement
range and identity criteria. The XRF provenance audit reproduced all
13,390 reported Trips~1--4 cells under the maximum-positive rule and
all 4,293 reported Trip~5 cells under the last-reported rule, with no
mismatched reported cells, and verified that the canonical laboratory
input comprises all 725 processed records. An independent
chemical-identifier validation evaluated all 93 instrument channels against
the pinned ChEBI release 247 and the checksummed PubChem snapshot of
23 July 2026 (Section~\ref{methods:xrf}); all
rows passed the declared rules, with explicit nulls for the aggregate
LE channel and unsupported oxides. The pH validation accounts for 1,168 specimen-assay entries and
reproduces the recorded admission and exclusion dispositions.
It checks specimen identity for every admitted measurement, including
field replicate~2 for all 156 Trip~4 measurements.


\subsection{Laboratory controls and measurement agreement}


The PowerSoil Pro extraction-control profile contained 1,165 ASVs,
with 84\% of reads assigned to ASVs shared with biological profiles.
Its nearest canonical profile was 46Dr1, with Bray--Curtis
dissimilarity 0.39. The frozen control adjudication interpreted this profile as contamination of the blank with soil template. Its recorded extraction-blank role and sequence record are retained, with a characterisation-only disposition; it is excluded from the extraction-blank prevalence screen to avoid treating the shared soil signal as reagent contamination. A library-order test compared nearby library numbers with
different-site pairs matched by geographic-distance decile and
compartment, using 999 library-order permutations. The Trip~1 and
Trip~3 Bray--Curtis contrasts were $-0.003$ ($p=0.15$) and
$+0.006$ ($p=0.99$), respectively. These contrasts summarize the
tested preparation-order association.

To assess agreement between field and laboratory XRF measurements,
repeated field sessions were reduced to site medians and paired
with the Trip~5 subsurface laboratory observation at 58 sites.
Among 12 analytes with positive values in both workflows at more
than 10 paired sites, the median Spearman correlation was 0.21
(range $-0.40$ to 0.48).
This diagnostic describes site-level rank agreement among the
retained positive pairs.

\subsection{Competency questions and SPARQL queries}

Competency questions define the intended retrieval tasks
(Table~\ref{tab:cq}). The archived query workload covers site,
geochemical, molecular-provenance and taxonomic data. The
Site~10 XRF query has an archived 46-row result. Source-derived
expected result tuples also specify the complete 48-observation
genus profile for run \texttt{ERR16061083},
360-row environment--taxon join, 70 site coordinates and six
PCR no-template control roles. The release validator compares full
result multisets, preserving duplicate rows and requiring exact
identifiers. Numeric comparisons use absolute and relative
tolerances of $10^{-12}$; coordinate comparisons use an absolute
tolerance of $10^{-10}$ degrees. The query comparisons test the links between observations and their
specimen, process and site context.

For KG v3.0.1, release validation verifies all 16 input-module hashes and
parses the complete 45,707,660-quad export, checking every graph context.
The terminology validation checks the term axioms and one definition per
annotation property for each of 342 project terms. Public-release checks
verify equivalent ontology content in RDF/XML, Turtle, JSON-LD and
N-Triples, resolution of the four reference imports, manifest integrity,
the live asserted-graph count and the deposited artifacts.
Section~\ref{records} identifies the validation evidence and its input
versions. Structural tests evaluate the scientific modules separately.

Regression fixtures execute the taxonomic and environmental queries in Usage Notes on deliberately confusable
inputs. The taxonomy fixture assigns the same genus different
abundances in two profiles and adds a species-rank observation; the
query returns exactly the two matching genus observations. The visit
fixture gives one site two visits at 40 and 20\degree C; the

\subsection{MAG catalogue integrity}

The integrity check compared the 5,438 refined MAG FASTAs against the representative inventory
and the refined-bin CheckM2 report. Every genome's contig count and summed
sequence length matched that report. The archive contains 2,485,945 contigs
totalling 20,490,828,267~bp. A SHA-256 checksum was computed for each FASTA,
and every archive member was reread and its bytes verified against the
per-genome manifest. The GTDB R232 classification tables passed their
upstream checksum checks and contained exactly the same 5,438 genome
identifiers. Library metadata reconciled all 551 read-bearing libraries
between the paired-read inventory and the sample-context table.

The gene annotations were checked against the same sequences. Before
annotating a genome, we verified its FASTA against the catalogue checksum.
We accepted an annotation only when every eggNOG query was a protein that
Bakta had predicted for that genome and each query occurred once. All
5,438 genomes passed, and the release step reread every annotation file
against its recorded checksum. Bakta predicted 19,186,792 proteins, a
median of 3,325 per genome. eggNOG-mapper annotated 84.0\% of these
proteins and assigned a KO to 44.4\%, and every genome has at least one
annotated protein. Across the catalogue, the annotations contain 11,386
distinct KOs, 9,703 Pfam domains and 141 CAZy families.

Each of the 551 coverage tables lists exactly the 5,438 catalogue genomes.
All values are finite and non-negative, covered fractions are at most one,
read counts are integers, and the reported length of each genome equals its
total sequence length in the catalogue. The median library aligned 45.9\%
of its reads to the catalogue (range 3.9--83.8\%). Seven libraries reached
the limit of 200 million read pairs. Library F5Dr1, which yielded no
assembly, contributed 616 read pairs and had zero mapped reads in the
relative-abundance cohort matrix (\texttt{EQ-COVERM-REL-ABUND-v1.0.0}).
Cohort reconciliation verified exact specimen, site, campaign, compartment
and dune-zone joins across the 550 non-zero mapped libraries, the 546
core-transect libraries across 59 primary sites, the 545 read-depth-modeled
core libraries, the 442 amplicon-matched libraries across 58 primary sites
and the 441 matched read-depth-modeled libraries, together with the strict
MIMAG contamination boundary separating the 5,437 ENA MAGs (126 high-quality
and 5,311 strict medium-quality representatives) from
\texttt{48Dr2\_\_self\_SemiBin\_25} (10.0\% contamination) and the separate
product provenance of the 20,451,986 Pyrodigal representative proteins
(\texttt{EQ-PROT-PYRODIGAL-REP-v1.0.0}), the frozen analytical KO matrix
(\texttt{EQ-KO-LEGACY-ANALYTICAL-v0}), and the frozen analytical taxonomy
snapshot (\texttt{EQ-TAX-LEGACY-ANALYTICAL-v0}, reconciled 1:1 against
canonical GTDB R232 strings across all 5,438 representatives with 1,898
differing strings) associated with the same 5,438
representative genome identifiers.

All 5,438 genomes completed gene calling, profile searches, rule evaluation and continuous-trait prediction. The aggregate tables contain each canonical genome identifier exactly once and retain its GTDB release 232 taxonomy and CheckM2 metrics. The three trait matrices contain 37, 100 and 189 trait categories, respectively, in addition to genome length and continuous predictions. A six-genome smoke test reproduced the official microTrait extraction outputs exactly before the complete cohort was processed. Separate coverage tests verify the missing phosphatase models and prevent unassayed alternatives from being interpreted as absent genes. These checks establish execution and identifier consistency; the biological accuracy of individual trait predictions remains dependent on the underlying models and recovered genome sequence.

\subsection{ShEx schema validation}

The archived ShEx run used the shapes described in
Section~\ref{subsec:schema_def}. Its tractable modules passed their
assigned shapes. The pH module was
validated separately over all 712 measurement processes, values and
acidity qualities and all 29 sessions, and the laboratory-control
module passed its shape and scientific-invariant checks. Four negative
fixtures each alter one constraint: missing site coordinates, an invalid
value datatype, reversed value-to-quality direction, or an XRF process
with neither input nor target. The released shapes reject all four
fixtures. The
multi-gigabyte taxonomy ABox remains outside this memory-bounded run;
it is instead parsed in full by an independent Turtle parser and
scanned by a streaming structural validator, which accounted for
44,528,482 triples in 5,953,690 subject blocks with zero structural
violations. This checks full-file syntax and the generator's declared structural
relationships. The separate listing validator checks printed Turtle
against the modules available for that run. Taxonomy and external
reference checks require the corresponding pinned payloads.

\subsection{Logical consistency}

ELK returned \texttt{true} for the tractable scientific-module subset
loaded for validation, including the ecosystem, pH and laboratory-control
modules. The checked subset excludes the taxonomy ABox and large reference
modules. Its execution metadata identify the loaded axioms and import
handling; class-hierarchy precomputation was omitted. The ontology uses \texttt{xsd:double} literals and constructs
outside ELK's supported fragment \cite{owl2profiles}; the check therefore
applies to the constructs processed by that configuration.

Validation of the separate finite-rule implementation uses synthetic examples for long
hierarchies, transitive cycles, both project property-chain lengths,
restriction and intersection classification, and PCR-control ancestry.
The tests compare bounded, class-partitioned execution with an
unbounded reference closure and check recovery from an interrupted
run. Release-specific checks compare the complete control-role
inventory with its source, retrieve consequences of the measurement
chains and require the inferred graph to be disjoint from the
asserted graph. Completion requires the zero-addition pass specified
in Section~\ref{subsec:owl_rdf}, followed by the explicit clash
diagnostics.

Label validation checks that each labelled project entity has exactly one
label. The evidence identifies the input modules, their hashes and validator
versions (Section~\ref{records}).
