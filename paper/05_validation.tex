\section{Technical validation}\label{valid}

Technical validation is organized around the evidential question each
test can answer. Source-to-graph reconciliation checks accounting; XRF
replicate and cross-workflow analyses check measurement behaviour;
ShEx checks graph structure; bounded ELK classification checks a
stated OWL fragment; and executable SPARQL checks a specific retrieval
path. Passing one layer is not reported as validation of another.

\subsection{Source reconciliation and measurement validation}

The executed evidence workflow produced a row-level sample ledger and
reconciliation summaries for specimens, extracts, libraries, runs and
community profiles. The 2,516 eligible source rows reconcile exactly
to 2,516 generated sample individuals; the 1,647 extraction-eligible
rows reconcile to 1,647 DNA extracts; and 1,242 resolved sequence rows
reconcile to 1,242 amplicon libraries and 1,242 FASTQ datasets. The
canonical feature table contains 1,271 profiles, while ecological
filtering retains 1,237: 1,227 from the sites~1--60 primary frame and
10 from the Trip-1-only sites~61--64. The profile-identity audit also
reproduced 29 pairs of export columns that collapse to the same field
identifier in the canonical table, found zero identical count-vector
pairs and verified that 28 pairs remain after ecological filtering.
This audit prevents the repeated identifiers from being mistaken for
identical copied columns; it cannot recover their undocumented upstream
laboratory or export relationship.

The site-alias gate independently matches each Trip~1 numeric
identifier 61--64 to a named catalogue individual by exact coordinates
and rejects coordinate or IRI disagreement. It accounts for all 36
affected source rows. The regenerated environmental module also
resolves all four coordinate-matched named Trip~1 visits, and the
sample graph links every affected specimen to both its canonical site
and the corresponding visit without changing the numeric source
identifier or repeated-campaign exclusion. An additional cross-module gate
checks all 1,004 sample-process \texttt{sio:000068} links against the
regenerated environmental module.
All 252 distinct visit targets resolve, and every linked visit targets the same
catalogue site as the sample process through \texttt{sio:000291}. This gate
prevents a sample module generated against an earlier visit numbering from
passing validation merely because it is internally well formed.

The environmental-metadata gate reproduced 274 nonblank source
records and verified all 24 exact-cell dispositions against the
corresponding raw cells. It retained the workbook-supported Trip~3
site~21 humidity of 31.321\%, restored 15 appended observations to
their original March 2023 dates, and confirmed that the resulting
Trip~3 expedition contains 65 February 2024 records ending on 21
February. All retained field temperatures, pressures and relative
humidities fell within their declared physical ranges. The unresolved
Trip~5 site~40 value of 194\% was preserved in the ledger but absent
from both the curated table and the regenerated environmental RDF
module. Regression tests also ensure that the Trip~2 termination note
cannot leak into a measurement column.
The regenerated module links 260 of the 274 rows to unambiguous
catalogue visits and contains 260 temperatures, 187 pressures and 251
relative-humidity values. Fourteen auxiliary or revisit labels remain
table-only because coordinate coincidence does not establish catalogue
identity; exact catalogue label \texttt{19.5} is the sole auxiliary
record linked to a visit.

The high-risk XRF inventory was independently counted by
\path{scripts/validation/audit_xrf_inventory.py} and reconciled by
\path{scripts/xrf/audit_xrf_provenance.py}. The audit reproduced
13,390 reported Trips~1--4 cells under the maximum-positive rule and
4,293 reported Trip~5 cells under the last-reported rule, with no
mismatched reported cells. It also verified that the canonical
laboratory input comprises all 725 processed records and that the
retired 158- and 705-row artifacts are absent from analytical
dependencies. The release-evidence and XRF regression suites comprised
automated source, aggregation and manuscript-consistency tests, all of
which passed for the staged modules.

The independent chemical-identifier gate
\path{scripts/xrf/audit_xrf_chemical_mapping.py} checked all 93
instrument channels against the checksum-recorded, pinned ChEBI v247
OWL file and a checksummed PubChem snapshot retrieved on 23 July
2026. All rows passed. The resulting ledger contains 79 supported ChEBI and 91
supported PubChem cross-references; audited nulls are retained for LE,
unsupported ChEBI oxides and the PrO$_2$ channel rather than being
replaced by lexical matches. The pinned ChEBI v247 and NCBITaxon OWL
source files are identified by checksum but are not redistributed;
re-executing the corresponding identifier gates requires reacquiring those
exact upstream versions.

The field/laboratory diagnostic is a site-level comparison, not a
same-aliquot validation. Missing machine-readable unit, detection-limit
and non-detect semantics limit absolute comparisons and interpretation
of zero values; structural graph validation does not resolve those
measurement limitations.

The pH reconciliation independently accounts for all 1,168 workbook rows and
matches every admitted row to exactly one existing specimen. It admits 712
values grouped into 29 reconstructed sessions and reproduces the five dispositions reported
in Data Records: 712 admitted, 356 pending, 45 depleted, 36 date-quarantined
and 19 quality-control-quarantined rows. Source size and checksum are verified
against the frozen version manifest. The cross-version gate reports 653
unchanged predecessor observations, 59 additions, and no absent, revised-value
or revised-metadata observation. Because measurement date contributes to the
stable entity key, a corrected date would be reported as one absent and one
added observation rather than as revised metadata; either outcome fails the
no-change gate. This validates version membership and source accounting, not
whether an unavailable specimen would have followed the same pH distribution.

\subsection{Structural validation}

We used Shape Expressions (ShEx) \cite{shex} for closed-world checks of
required properties, cardinalities and datatypes. The current site,
specimen, environmental-measurement, DNA, XRF, sequence-record and QC
modules passed their assigned shapes. The pH gate additionally validates every
one of the 712 measurement processes, values and acidity qualities, together
with all 29 sessions, against \path{shex/ph_measurements.shex}. The deposited
\path{evidence/ph/ph_validation.shexmap} enumerates all 2,167 node--shape
pairs. A minimal negative graph with UO:0000196 removed from one pH value,
its shape map and the nonconformance log are distributed alongside it under
\path{evidence/ph/}. The canonical
Turtle and RDF/XML files are graph-equivalent and the root knowledge graph
imports their shared version IRI. This establishes conformance of
the tested nodes to the declared schemas, not source accuracy,
completeness, absence of contamination or biological plausibility.

The multi-gigabyte taxonomy ABox remains outside the memory-bounded
full-ShEx run. It is instead generated in an isolated workflow task,
checksum-identified, parsed in full by an independent Turtle parser and
scanned by a memory-bounded structural validator. That scan requires
every generated classification process, absolute and relative dataset,
abundance quality and abundance value to have the expected types,
directions and cardinalities; it also reconciles the complete generated
identifier ranges and rejects a truncated file. The resulting evidence
is full-file syntax and generator-specific structural validation, not a
claim that the taxonomy ABox passed the unexecuted ShEx shapes.
The tractable modules' ShEx suite tests both conformance and rejection.
Three positive fixtures (a georeferenced sampling site, a canonical
climate measurement and a laboratory-XRF process with a specimen input)
must conform, and four negative fixtures must be rejected by the
released shapes: a sampling site without \texttt{geo:asWKT}, a
measurement payload whose value carries an invalid datatype, a
measurement in which the quality rather than the value is the subject
of \texttt{sio:000215}, and an XRF process with neither an input nor a
target. Each negative fixture differs from its positive counterpart in
exactly one respect, so a passing negative test identifies the
constraint that caught it. A further positive fixture exercises the complete
laboratory-control pattern, and two single-defect control fixtures reject an
extraction blank realized at the wrong processing stage and an expected-taxon
assertion whose composition specification lacks source provenance. The suite
is \path{tests/shex_negatives/run_tests.groovy}; all ten fixtures behave
as declared. The coverage is deliberate rather than exhaustive: it
targets the four defect classes that the modules are structurally able
to admit, and it does not enumerate every constraint in every shape.
A separate query gate,
\path{scripts/validation/validate_site_biome_completeness.py}, supplies
the closed-world check that no shape can express, requiring each of the
70 sampling-site individuals to carry a biome and an
environmental-feature restriction with ENVO fillers.
In the final workstation run, the structural scan read all
6,722,670,815 bytes and 50,482,175 lines of the file whose SHA-256 is
\texttt{d78be36a0fb3f91a28bec4c6489ca1a34d97c576e10cd2c92fc59e4dfd0eb808}.
A reader should expect that digest and that byte count for the
deposited \path{ontology/rubalkhali_taxonomy_abox.ttl}; the staged
deposit copy and the generated module both carry it. The scan accounted
for 44,528,482
triples and 5,953,690 subject blocks with zero structural violations
or warnings. Raptor independently parsed the complete Turtle file and
reported the same 44,528,482 triples with no diagnostic warning or
error. The two passes also reconciled all 1,236 processes, 17,304
datasets, 2,962,918 qualities and 2,962,918 values.

Before ABox generation, the complete taxonomy mapping passed coverage,
rank, exact-label-or-synonym, NCBI-ancestry, contextual-IRI-collision
and shared-prefix-consistency checks. The builder quarantined the
single historical source-header artifact, covered all 4,305 canonical
lineages with 30,135 rank rows and reported zero missing lineages. It
also verified graph equivalence of the 55,628-triple Turtle and RDF/XML
taxonomy modules and rejected identity axioms or any reparenting of an
NCBI class beneath a local class. These checks validate the stated
mapping policy and serialization contract; they do not turn a
contextual class into an externally identified taxon.

\subsection{Formal-semantic validation}

ELK classification is limited to OWL~2~EL \cite{elk}. The gate runs ELK
0.4.3 through the OWL API 4.5.26. A clean 1~August 2026 workstation
rerun on the current tractable modules loaded 781,293 axioms from 10
distinct ontology IRIs using local module and ontology caches; sibling
serializations sharing an ontology IRI were deduplicated. ELK reported
the resulting merged ontology as consistent. The run excluded the
multi-gigabyte taxonomy ABox and omitted the retired pre-audit
project-taxonomy and NCBI-subset modules; the generated canonical
ecosystem and laboratory-control modules were included. It did not precompute
the class hierarchy. A separate label gate merged 782,229 triples from 11
current files and confirmed exactly one label for each of 104,697 labelled
\texttt{RAK\_} subjects. All assigned ShEx checks and the IRI-registry
gate also passed, as did the control-specific invariant validator. The exact
log, input hashes and validator hashes are archived under
\path{evidence/semantic-validation/}.

A separate 1~August 2026 control-semantic run validated every shaped node in
the 3,800-triple control module, not a sample of nodes. It confirmed 489
registered entities, 18 expected-taxon assertions for 18 manufacturer
composition rows, graph equivalence of the Turtle and RDF/XML serializations,
the four declared SIO inverse pairs, the ShEx-required input, target and
output links, generator determinism and merged bounded
ELK consistency. Its machine-readable record, log and hashes are archived in
the same evidence directory.

This result is not a full OWL~2~DL consistency claim. ELK is incomplete
for universal restrictions, inverse properties and other constructs
used by imported modules, and the excluded ABoxes were not covered.
Likewise, the selected forward-rule program has not been demonstrated
to reach a fixed point and is not described as complete OWL-Horst
closure \cite{owlhorst}.

\subsection{Worked-example validation}

The worked Turtle patterns of Section~\ref{ontology} are the part of
this descriptor a reuser is most likely to copy, so they are checked
mechanically rather than maintained by hand.
\path{scripts/validation/verify_manuscript_listings.py} reads the
prefix table out of the manuscript, parses each Turtle listing, and
resolves every resulting triple against the staged modules, including
the \texttt{owl:Restriction} structures that the sampling-site pattern
depends on and the multi-gigabyte taxonomy ABox. It additionally
requires every prefixed IRI named in the section prose to exist in the
graph, which is what catches a malformed term or a property that was
never minted. The current listing gate resolves all 99 printed triples
across the nine Turtle listings and all 55 prose IRIs. A drifted listing
therefore fails a test instead of reaching a reader as an unusable
query pattern.

\subsection{Executable field-XRF query validation}

Listing~\ref{lst:sparql_xrf} follows the corrected field-XRF path. The
process targets the recorded site with \texttt{sio:000291}; it does
not claim a physical specimen input. The produced value points to its
quality with \texttt{sio:000215}. The exact query is archived as
\path{sparql/field_xrf_site10.rq}. The workflow executed
\path{scripts/validation/validate_competency_query.py} against the
checksummed base, site and XRF modules and required the fixed expected
cardinality of 46 analyte-value rows. It returned exactly 46 rows for
Site~10: 24 from field process Test~5847 and 22 from Test~5848. The
validator also required exactly two process labels and no site label
other than Site~10, and wrote the result table, input checksums and
machine-readable validation record under
\path{evidence/competency-query/}.

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Retrieve field-XRF analyte values for a
    selected site.}, label={lst:sparql_xrf},
  basicstyle=\ttfamily\scriptsize, frame=single]
PREFIX rak: <https://rubalkhali.science/kb/RAK_>
PREFIX sio: <http://semanticscience.org/resource/SIO_>
PREFIX rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#>

SELECT ?processLabel ?siteLabel ?analyte ?concentration
WHERE {
  ?process a rak:0000025 ;
           rdfs:label ?processLabel ;
           sio:000291 ?site ;
           sio:000229 ?value .
  FILTER(CONTAINS(LCASE(STR(?processLabel)), "field xrf"))
  ?site rdfs:label ?siteLabel .
  FILTER(?siteLabel = "Site 10")
  ?value sio:000215 ?quality ;
         rak:2000012 ?concentration .
  ?quality a ?qualityClass .
  ?qualityClass rdfs:label ?analyte .
  FILTER(?qualityClass != rak:0000032)
}
ORDER BY ?processLabel ?analyte
\end{lstlisting}

This establishes only the archived, bounded field-XRF retrieval and
its 46-row cardinality. No broader competency-query pass rate is
claimed: several historical queries lacked fixed expected
cardinalities, and the unbounded cross-run taxonomy query exceeded its
development timeout. Those queries are not used as technical-validation
evidence in this descriptor.
