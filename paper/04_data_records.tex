\section{Data records}\label{records}

\subsection{Record types and accounting}

The resource distinguishes a field specimen from an extraction,
library, sequence run, quality-controlled community profile and
derived observation. The resource includes a machine-readable ledger
linking
\begin{center}
\small
site and trip $\rightarrow$ specimen $\rightarrow$ extraction
$\rightarrow$ library\\
$\rightarrow$ sequence run $\rightarrow$ QC decision
$\rightarrow$ derived community.
\end{center}
The versioned ledger contains 2,550 source rows: 2,302 master-sheet
rows (2,268 non-control and 34 control rows) plus 248 plant rows. Of these, 2,540
pass the metadata-completeness audit, and all 2,516 non-control rows
currently satisfy the knowledge-graph sample-ingest rule. The ten rows
that fail the completeness audit are all Trip~1 master-sheet laboratory
controls (\texttt{Sample\_Mastersheet.xlsx}, sheet \texttt{Trip1}, rows
578--587): six reagent controls, two PCR controls and two extraction
controls. They fail because a control carries no site and no
compartment, not because a field record is damaged, and their ledger
exclusion reason is \texttt{control\_requires\_explicit\_control\_model}.
They are retained in the ledger with their source identifiers and sheet
row numbers. They are not promoted to field specimens; the separate
control ABox represents evidence-supported control materials, roles,
processing batches, sequence occurrences, assertions and unresolved
dispositions. The generated field-specimen
graph contains 2,516 sample individuals, 1,647 DNA-extract individuals, 1,242 amplicon
libraries and 1,242 FASTQ datasets. The canonical Trips~1--5 feature
table contains 1,271 profiles (1,242 unique field identifiers),
whereas the ecological analysis retains 1,237 profiles (1,209 unique
field identifiers) across 64 numeric sites. Ten of those retained
profiles belong to Trip-1-only sites~61--64; the primary
repeated-campaign analysis therefore contains 1,227 profiles from
sites~1--60.
The 29 extra profiles in the canonical feature table arise from 29
normalized identifiers that each occur in two export columns. A
count-vector audit found that none of those 29 pairs is identical. After
ecological filtering, both profiles remain for 28 of the identifiers.
Because the available provenance does not establish whether these are
technical reruns, separate aliquots or another export mechanism, the
profiles remain distinct and are not silently averaged or deduplicated.
An older 1,013-profile table remains in the archive staging tree for
provenance, but the package manifest marks it and its companion
taxonomy table as legacy artifacts excluded from the canonical
dataset. The canonical Trips~1--5 feature table is staged separately
and checksum-verified.

The 34 rows not ingested as specimens are all laboratory controls and
are referenced by the separate control model rather than forced through
the field-specimen shape. The 36 genuine Trip-1-only
records from numeric sites 61--64 are ingested through the
coordinate-confirmed alias ledger: their source identifiers are
preserved while their specimens and visits link to the four existing
named site individuals. These rows contribute 11 profiles to the canonical feature table and,
after ecological filtering, 10 profiles to the ecological table; the two
numbers refer to the two different tables and are not a discrepancy.
They remain excluded from
repeated-campaign inference because the sites could not be revisited.
After applying the confirmed metadata corrections and aliases and
regenerating the sample and DNA modules, eligible-source/sample and
expected/generated-DNA counts reconcile exactly. The remaining 26
SRA rows outside the field-specimen lineage are all control-labelled
records (18
\texttt{EB}, six \texttt{Negative} and two \texttt{T\_Neg} records)
and are represented, where evidence permits, as control sequence
occurrences rather than specimen-linked runs. The 34 feature-table
profiles absent from the ecological table comprise 24 controls, nine
biological-profile identifiers below the declared 1,000-read ecological
threshold and one additional \texttt{T1Dr1} run with 934 reads, also below
that threshold.

\subsection{Taxonomy mapping records}

The combined taxonomy source contains 354,251 feature assignments in
4,305 distinct seven-rank lineages; 351,472 of those features occur in
the canonical 1,271-profile feature table. Coverage is complete in the
direction that matters for reuse: every one of the 351,472 canonical
features carries a lineage, and none is unclassified. The remaining
2,779 assignments describe features that have no column entry in the
canonical table and no entry in the retired legacy table either, so they
contribute no counts to any analysis. The pipeline step that dropped
them is not recorded in the exported artifacts, so they are retained as
classified-but-uncounted provenance rather than explained away. Expanding each lineage over
the seven ranks yields 30,135 mapping-row occurrences but only 8,133
distinct rank-truncated source contexts. Of those occurrences, 20,045
use validated NCBI identifiers, 2,509 use one of 592 unambiguous
historical project identifiers, and 7,581 use deterministic contextual
identifiers. The latter correspond to 5,030 unique contextual classes.
Four historical project identifiers reused across incompatible
contexts (36 explicit historical rows) are not retained; all their
affected contexts receive lineage-scoped identifiers. No additional
project identifier required demotion after the corrected-parent
fixed-point audit.

The clean taxonomy module declares the 5,622 local classes (592
retained and 5,030 contextual) plus 2,666 NCBI classes needed for the
complete native ancestor closure. Its RDF/XML and Turtle
serializations are graph-equivalent at 55,628 triples. The 119
non-identical supplementary species calls comprise 98 calls where the
seventh rank was empty and 21 that conflicted with a populated
seventh-rank value; all remain in the provenance ledger and none is
promoted into the asserted lineage.

The checksum-frozen abundance ABox is 6,722,670,815 bytes and contains
44,528,482 triples in 5,953,690 contiguous subject blocks. It
represents 1,236 feature profiles with resolvable FASTQ individuals as
1,236 processing individuals and 17,304 absolute or relative datasets
(14 per process). Across seven ranks it records 2,962,918 abundance
qualities and 2,962,918 corresponding value individuals, divided
evenly between absolute and relative representations (1,481,459 of
each quality and value type). The ABox has 1,236 FASTQ bearer subjects
and 8,056 taxon bearer subjects. Controls and the 11 Trip-1-only
feature-table profiles from sites~61--64, for which no SRA run is
recorded, are not
silently assigned to taxonomy processes or FASTQ bearers. Control
sequence occurrences and unresolved mappings are encoded in the
separate control ABox; the Trip-1-only sample and site individuals are
encoded in the field modules. Their source observations remain in the
release ledger and taxonomy source tables.

\subsection{Environmental field records}

The five immutable field sheets and the original supporting workbook
are distributed with a 24-row exact-cell correction ledger. The
generated canonical table contains 274 nonblank field records. Its
dispositions comprise eight Trip~2 column shifts, 15 workbook-supported
campaign-date restorations and one quarantined out-of-range Trip~5
humidity observation. The machine-readable audit records the input
hashes, applied ledger entries, record roles, measurement counts,
physical ranges and generated-output hashes. The supplementary
environmental table and the environmental RDF module consume the same
ledger; neither contains the invalid 194\% humidity assertion.
The table retains all 274 records, while the RDF module links 260
visits to catalogue sites. Fourteen auxiliary/revisit rows with
non-catalogue labels remain table-only rather than being assigned by
coordinate coincidence. The graph-linked coverage is 260 temperature,
187 pressure and 251 humidity values.

\subsection{Archived-soil pH records}

The pH distribution is versioned as \texttt{EQ-PH-SHARED-v1.0.0}. The frozen
source workbook, manifest, version registry and retained predecessor are under
\path{metadata/samples/ph/}. The row-level audit, admitted-measurement table,
29 reconstructed-session table and stable entity registry are under
\path{metadata/ph/}.
Together they account for 1,168 source rows: 712 admitted measurements, 356
pending rows, 45 depleted specimens, 36 date quarantines and 19 numeric rows
quarantined for incomplete recorded quality control. The source notes identify
\texttt{S28Sr1} as missed during preparation and \texttt{S57Dr1} as depleted;
neither receives an imputed value.

The canonical pH module is distributed as graph-equivalent RDF/XML and Turtle
files named \path{ontology/rubalkhali_ph_eq_ph_shared_v1_0_0.owl} and
\path{ontology/rubalkhali_ph_eq_ph_shared_v1_0_0.ttl}. It contains 712
measuring processes, 712 acidity qualities, 712 pH value individuals and 29
sessions. The corresponding source reconciliation, complete ecology
sensitivity, predecessor comparison, Shape Map result and output checksums are
under \path{evidence/ph/}. In particular,
\path{evidence/ph/ecology/ph_sample_profile_join.tsv} records the 702 exact
specimen--profile joins,
\path{evidence/ph/ecology/ph_group_analysis_table.tsv} records the 562
site--campaign--position means,
\path{evidence/ph/ecology/ph_geographic_adjustment.tsv} records the
same-cohort spatial comparison, and
\path{evidence/ph/ecology/ph_influence_sensitivity.tsv} records the singleton
maximum and whole-site exclusions. The composition analysis uses the 558
groups reported in \path{evidence/ph/ecology/ph_composition_models.tsv}. Of
the predecessor's 653 admitted observations, all 653 are unchanged and retain
their IRIs; the shared version adds 59 admitted observations.

\subsection{Knowledge-graph modules}

The distribution is organized into reusable modules for the base
terminology, sites, specimens, environmental and climate
measurements, XRF, DNA extractions, sequence records, sequence QC and
taxonomic observations, plus separate laboratory-control and archived-soil pH
modules.
The staging package contains the generated
modules, the source metadata included in the ledger, RDF generators,
ShEx schemas, validation scripts, and the generated release and XRF
evidence.

Tabular records are tab-separated with a header row and no index
column, except for the explicitly named portable CSV samplesheet; RDF
modules use the prefixes of Table~\ref{tab:prefixes}.
\path{metadata/DATA_DICTIONARY.tsv} records the field or field pattern,
data type, unit, missing-value convention and definition for curated
canonical tabular records; raw instrument exports retain their source
headers. Quantities carry units only where a unit is
documented at the source. Environmental source tables retain degrees
Celsius, hectopascals (millibars) and percent relative humidity. In
\path{ontology/rubalkhali_measurements.owl}, pressure magnitudes are
multiplied by 100 and asserted in pascals
(\texttt{obo:UO\_0000110}); climate records use degrees Celsius and
millimetres; DNA concentrations are in nanograms per microlitre. Archived-soil
pH values use UO:0000196 and are bound to the specified CaCl$_2$ protocol. XRF
concentrations carry no documented unit or unit assertion and must not
be treated as percentages.

The package-level manifest \path{PRE_RELEASE_MANIFEST.tsv} is
exhaustive over payload files: it has one row for each candidate payload
file, and the
builder fails if a declared file is absent or a present file is
unclassified, so the manifest cannot drift from the tree. Each row
carries the relative path, a category, a release status
(\texttt{canonical-candidate}, \texttt{audit-evidence},
\texttt{legacy-excluded}, \texttt{derived-review-required} or
\texttt{operational-reference}), a one-line record scope, the byte
size, the SHA-256 checksum, and a licence status. Licences are recorded
as open gates rather than asserted: project-produced files await the
depositor's licence selection, while redistributed content derived from
NCBI Taxonomy, the imported OBO ontologies or the SILVA reference
database retains a separate third-party licence gate tied to the pinned
upstream release. Legacy taxonomy and XRF
tables remain available for provenance but are explicitly excluded
from canonical analytical dependencies.

Scope boundaries of the deposit, stated once: it contains no raw
sequencing reads (these are in the sequence archives, see
Section~\ref{data_availability}), no metagenome-assembled-genome
catalogue, and no assembly or binning intermediates. The canonical
feature table contains 24 extraction-blank community profiles. The
package records the author-confirmed product, stage and assay facts in
\path{evidence/controls/control_ground_truth.tsv}; normalized control
registries and process tables in \path{metadata/controls/}; the
generated SIO-patterned control graph in
\path{ontology/rubalkhali_controls.ttl}; the candidate-ASV,
expected-recovery and exact-ASV-overlap records in
\path{evidence/control-audit/}; and all 25 before/after headline
metrics across 11 claims in
\path{evidence/control-sensitivity/headline_result_sensitivity.tsv}.
The control graph contains 489 entities and 3,800 triples, including 51
material-specific role individuals and 47 sequence occurrences. It
represents a control as a material bearing a role realized in a
laboratory process, links extraction processes to batches rather than
trips, and expresses uncertain identities or mappings as
evidence-bearing dispositions instead of ground truth.

The downloadable reviewer archive omits three payloads above its
256-MiB threshold and lists them with byte size and SHA-256 checksum in its
separate bulk manifest:
\path{metadata/metagenome/eq.emapper.annotations.gz},
\path{metadata/taxonomy/feature-table-trips1-5.tsv}, and
\path{ontology/rubalkhali_taxonomy_abox.ttl}. A complete deposit must supply
those three byte-identical files; their omission is only a reviewer-download
packaging choice.
