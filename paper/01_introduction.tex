\section{Background and summary}\label{intro}

The Rub' al-Khali (Empty Quarter) is a hyper-arid sand desert that spans approximately 500,000 km$^2$ of the southern Arabian Peninsula \cite{mandaville_1986}. Its landscape is dominated by extensive linear dunes known as \textit{'uruq} (singular \textit{`irq}, Arabic for ``vein''), interspersed with gravel plains, salt flats, and scattered shrub communities \cite{edgell2006arabian,mandaville_1986}. Environmental conditions are characterized by large temperature fluctuations, irregular rainfall, and frequent dust storms \cite{nelli2024wind}. Despite these harsh conditions, soil microbial communities also inhabit this extreme environment \cite{makhalanyane2015}, where spatial variation in soil properties and temporal changes in environmental conditions may influence their composition and distribution. Understanding these communities requires observations that account for both the spatial heterogeneity of the landscape and its changing environmental conditions.

Previous studies of soils in Saudi Arabia have surveyed microbial communities along elevation gradients \cite{yasir2015saudi}, responses to rainfall across soil compartments \cite{aslam2016rainfall}, and differences between bulk and rhizosphere soils across regions \cite{khan2020saudi}. A study investigated microbial communities associated with desert plants \cite{eida2018plants}. Repeated sampling across sites, soil compartments, and time points provides an opportunity to extend this work by examining microbial communities across both spatial and temporal scales. Integrating data at such a scale introduces data management requirements as it becomes necessary to preserve the relationship between sampling locations, collection events, laboratory analyses, and environmental measurements.

Several resources provide models for organizing environmental and microbiome observations. Multi-campaign initiatives, such as Tara Oceans, link campaigns, stations, and sampling events to environmental and sequence data \cite{taraoceans2015}. MIxS/MIMARKS specify contextual metadata for environmental sequencing data \cite{mixs}. The National Microbiome Data Collaborative promotes the use of persistent identifiers and shared schemas to connect microbiome datasets \cite{nmdc2026}. Knowledge graphs, including KG-Microbe \cite{kgmicrobe2026} and MetagenomicKG \cite{metagenomickg2026}, provide complementary approaches for integrating taxonomic, functional and environmental information. Building on these principles, this resource provides a linked representation of field samples, sampling events, laboratory processes, and environmental measurements from a repeated survey of the Rub' al-Khali.

The resource covers five campaigns between 2023 and 2025 along a 1,043~km transect across the Saudi Rub' al-Khali. Repeatedly sampled soil specimens connect amplicon profiles and shotgun metagenomes to a catalogue of 5,438 species-representative metagenome-assembled genomes. Field observations, archived-soil pH, X-ray fluorescence and climate data provide environmental context. Shared specimen identifiers connect these assays while retaining their different sampling coverage and processing histories. The Data Records section and the machine-readable cohort manifest distinguish the released biological products from the subsets used in each companion analysis.

To improve interoperability and enable integrated queries across heterogeneous data sources, the resource is represented using established ontologies and standardized identifiers. Sites, samples, processes, qualities, and measurement values are represented using the Semanticscience Integrated Ontology (SIO) \cite{sio}. This representation explicitly links sequencing results and environmental measurements to the samples and collection events they derive from, retaining their connections to the underlying data-generation processes. Chemical entities are encoded with ChEBI \cite{chebi}, environmental classifications with ENVO \cite{envo}, and taxonomic identifiers with NCBI Taxonomy \cite{ncbitaxon}. The resulting linked RDF modules support queries across sampling campaigns, samples, microbial communities, and environmental observations while preserving the context in which each observation was generated.

Technical validation assesses the consistency and integrity of the resource, including reconciliation with source data, measurement inclusion, identifier mappings, and structural constraints. Query-result comparisons assess whether the represented links recover the expected source observations. The reported scope distinguishes the ontology constructs and modules processed by the ELK reasoner \cite{elk}. The portal (\url{https://rubalkhali.science/}) provides site-level pages and a SPARQL endpoint for querying the resource. The Data Records section describes the available datasets, and Data Availability specifies their access conditions. By preserving specimen identity across amplicon profiles, shotgun metagenomes, refined genomes, gene calls, and geochemical measurements, this resource provides the shared foundation for companion manuscripts on landscape-scale and compartment community replacement and encoded persistence traits \cite{ecology_companion} and on shallow-subsurface archaeal unknown and export-enriched protein repertoires \cite{proteome_companion}.
