\section{Background and summary}\label{intro}

The Rub' al-Khali (Empty Quarter) is a hyper-arid sand sea of
approximately 500,000 km$^2$ across the southern Arabian Peninsula
\cite{mandaville_1986}. Air temperatures recorded during these
expeditions span 20.0--49.4\degree C across 273 field observations,
and the region receives negligible and highly irregular rainfall. Its
microbial communities remain poorly characterized relative to other
drylands \cite{makhalanyane2015}. To predict ecosystem responses to
climate change and to inform conservation and land-use policy,
researchers require deep knowledge of the biogeochemistry of such
extreme environments \cite{fierer2017}.

Saudi Arabia already has a substantial arid-soil microbiome
literature. Previous studies examined elevation gradients
\cite{yasir2015saudi}, rainfall responses across soil compartments
\cite{aslam2016rainfall}, open and rhizosphere soils across three
regions \cite{khan2020saudi}, rhizosphere bacteria of pioneer sand
plants near Jizan \cite{osman2016jizan}, and bacteria associated with
native desert plants \cite{eida2018plants}. More recent surveys characterized
fertility islands and plant-associated networks
\cite{maurice2023fertility,maurice2024}, sandy soils near natural and
artificial water points \cite{alawthan2024reserve}, rhizosphere and
endosphere communities across northern reserves
\cite{alhasani2026reserve}, and Red Sea coastal sabkhas
\cite{gopi2026sabkha}. Fungal studies progressed from four
western-coastal composite samples and four Saudi open-sand samples
\cite{moussa2017fungi,murgia2019sandfungi} to a standardized
single-visit survey of 85 locations across Saudi Arabia
\cite{mani2026mycobiome}. A recent synthesis likewise identifies
substantial geographic and taxonomic gaps in Arabian biological-soil-crust
research \cite{blancosacristan2025biocrust}. In the United Arab Emirates,
related studies
profiled a Rub' al-Khali interdune microbial mat \cite{mckay2016} and
desert-plant rhizosphere and endosphere communities \cite{mousa2024}.
This resource does not claim the first Saudi, national-scale or
arid-soil survey. It connects a repeated five-expedition bacterial
transect to joined provenance across locations, physical specimens,
molecular products, environmental observations, climate, and distinct
field and laboratory geochemistry workflows.

To characterize complex ecosystems, researchers must integrate
heterogeneous and multi-scale data: microbial community profiles
derived from DNA sequencing, elemental soil composition from
geochemical analyses, spatiotemporal environmental variables, and
provenance metadata that link physical samples to their digital
representations. Traditional approaches store these data types in
separate, disconnected repositories, which limits cross-domain queries
and reduces reproducibility.

Community reporting and infrastructure efforts provide important
precedents. The FAIR principles set the reuse target that a deposited
resource is judged against \cite{fair}. MIxS/MIMARKS define contextual
fields for marker-gene and
environmental sequence data \cite{mixs}, and the 67-item STREAMS
guideline extends reporting practice to environmental and
non-human-associated microbiomes \cite{streams2025}. The National
Microbiome Data Collaborative (NMDC) demonstrates how persistent
identifiers, standardized metadata, linked-data schemas, and
reproducible workflows can support reuse across environmental
microbiome studies \cite{nmdc2026}. Three vocabularies cover parts of
what an expedition resource must express: PROV-O models derivation and
activity \cite{provo}, OBI models investigations and assays
\cite{obi}, and SOSA/SSN models observations and their procedures
\cite{sosa}. None of them, on its own, carries a field specimen
through extraction, library preparation, sequencing, quality control
and taxon observation while keeping the geochemical and climate
measurements attached to the same site individual. The residual gap
after MIxS, STREAMS and NMDC is therefore not vocabulary coverage but
the joined instantiation: a single graph in which every one of those
records resolves to the same specimen and the same location.

Knowledge graphs address a complementary integration problem.
KG-Microbe builds modular graphs that connect microbial taxa with
traits, environments, and growth preferences
\cite{kgmicrobe2026}. MetagenomicKG integrates seven taxonomic,
functional, pathogen, and biomedical sources for metagenomic
applications \cite{metagenomickg2026}. These resources establish that
the existence of a microbiome knowledge graph is not itself novel.
The contribution of the present resource is instead the joined,
expedition-level provenance of locations, physical specimens,
laboratory processes, geochemistry, climate, sequence runs, quality
control, and taxon observations from a single landscape survey.

Constructing that resource requires a unified representation of
heterogeneous instruments and protocols, preservation of original
taxonomic assertions alongside harmonized identifiers, structural
validation at ingest, and an explicit account of which entailments
and queries are actually supported. We use persistent nodes for
samples, processes, qualities, quantities, and digital records so that
data users can traverse the recorded provenance chain instead of
reconstructing it from unrelated tables. The chain is documented where
the source identifiers permit it: the field XRF log carries no physical
specimen identifier, and the duplicated export columns described in
Section~\ref{records} have no recorded upstream relationship, so those
links are absent rather than inferred.

The dataset covers five field expeditions (2023--2025) along a
transect of more than 1,000~km across the Saudi Rub' al-Khali. The
intended repeated frame comprises numeric sites~1--60; four additional
numeric identifiers were used only on Trip~1, and the site catalogue
contains named special-interest locations. Coordinate matching,
confirmed against the field records, establishes that numeric
identifiers 61--64 denote four of those named catalogue locations.
We retain the numeric identifiers as source provenance but do not
count them as four additional physical locations. The current source
ledger contains 2,550 rows: 2,302 master-sheet rows (2,268 non-control
and 34 control rows) plus 248 plant rows. These are source records
rather than a claim that 2,550
distinct specimens have entered every downstream product. Sequence
records, quality-controlled community profiles, and derived taxonomic
observations are distinguished from physical specimens throughout
this descriptor. The generated graph contains 2,516 sample
individuals. Ecological filtering retains 1,237 profiles across 64
numeric site labels, four of which are Trip-1-only aliases of named
catalogue locations rather than additional physical sites: 1,227 from the primary repeated-campaign frame of
sites~1--60 and 10 Trip-1-only profiles from sites~61--64. Site measurements and
Open-Meteo climate records \cite{openmeteo} are linked at the
site--expedition level.

The geochemistry comprises two complementary X-ray fluorescence
(XRF) workflows. During Trip~5, the field log contains 106 in-situ
handheld entries across 59 sites; 71 complete sessions at 58 sites
were retained, including nine sites with repeated complete sessions.
The log has no physical-sample identifier. In the laboratory, archived
material from all five trips was represented by 725 processed records:
547 from Trips~1--4 and 178 from Trip~5. All 725 enter the canonical
laboratory analysis. A former 158-row Trip~5 table and the resulting
705-row cross-trip table are retired legacy artifacts and are not
analytical inputs.

The knowledge graph uses the Semanticscience Integrated Ontology
(SIO) \cite{sio} as its upper-level framework. Domain-specific
concepts are represented as subclasses of SIO entities, and
measurements follow SIO's process--quantity--quality pattern.
Chemical entities are grounded where supported in ChEBI
\cite{chebi}, environmental context in ENVO \cite{envo}, and
taxonomic identifiers in NCBI Taxonomy \cite{ncbitaxon}. Shape
Expressions test the structural contract of generated RDF. ELK tests
the OWL~2~EL portion of the terminology; it is not presented as a
complete OWL~2~DL consistency check. A selected forward-rule
materialization supports common query paths, and its coverage is
reported separately from standard OWL-Horst closure.

The companion ecological analysis reports the inferential results for
the quality-controlled community profiles, laboratory-XRF gradient,
rainfall, spatial turnover, PMA experiment, association networks and
genome-encoded functional potential \cite{ecology_companion}. The
present paper describes the reusable data products, provenance model,
identifier mappings and their executable validation.

Environmental digital twins are normally dynamic systems that
assimilate observations and support state estimation, simulation, or
forecasting \cite{khan2025,islam2026,ovaskainen2026}. The artifact
described here does not yet have those capabilities. It is a
versioned dataset and semantic backbone on which such a future system
could be built; the deposited version becomes immutable only once it is
archived under a persistent identifier. Its immediate purposes are data
discovery, provenance inspection, and reproducible reuse. It is
designed to support cross-domain querying; the executed and archived
query evidence at present is the bounded field-XRF retrieval of
Section~\ref{valid}.
