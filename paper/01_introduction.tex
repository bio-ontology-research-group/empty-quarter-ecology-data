\section{Background and summary}\label{intro}

The Rub' al-Khali (Empty Quarter) is a hyper-arid sand desert that spans approximately 500,000 km$^2$ of the southern Arabian Peninsula \cite{mandaville_1986}. Its landscape is dominated by extensive linear dunes known as \textit{'uruq} (singular \textit{`irq}, Arabic for ``vein''), interspersed with gravel plains, salt flats, and scattered shrub communities \cite{edgell2006arabian,mandaville_1986}. Environmental conditions are characterized by large temperature fluctuations, irregular rainfall, and frequent dust storms \cite{nelli2024wind}. Despite these harsh conditions, soil microbial communities also inhabit this extreme environment \cite{makhalanyane2015}, where spatial variation in soil properties and temporal changes in environmental conditions may influence their composition and distribution. Understanding these communities requires observations that account for both the spatial heterogeneity of the landscape and its changing environmental conditions.

Previous studies of soils in Saudi Arabia have surveyed microbial communities along elevation gradients \cite{yasir2015saudi}, reponses to rainfall across soil compartments \cite{aslam2016rainfall}, and differences between bulk and rhizosphere soils across regions \cite{khan2020saudi}. A study investigated microbial communities associated with desert plants \cite{eida2018plants}. Repeated sampling across sites, soil compartments, and time points provides an opportunity to extend this work by examining microbial communities across both spatial and temporal scales. Integrating data at such a scale introduces data management requirements as it becomes necessary to preserve the relationship between sampling locations, collection events, laboratory analyses, and environmental measurements.

Several resources provide models for organizing environmental and microbiome observations. Multi-campaign initiatives, such as Tara Oceans, link campaigns, stations, and sampling events to environmental and sequence data \cite{taraoceans2015}. MIxS/MIMARKS specify contextual metadata for environmental sequencing data \cite{mixs}. The National Microbiome Data Collaborative promotes the use of persistent identifiers and shared schemas to connect microbiome datasets \cite{nmdc2026}. Knowledge graphs, including KG-Microbe \cite{kgmicrobe2026} and MetagenomicKG \cite{metagenomickg2026}, provide complementary approaches for integrating taxonomic, functional and environmental information. Building on these preinciples, we present a linked representation of field samples, sampling events, laboratory processes, and environmental measurements from a repeated survey of the Rub' al-Khali.

We collected soil samples during five campaigns between 2023 and 2025 along a 1,043~km transect from the west to the east of the Rub' al-Khali in Saudi Arabia. The survey includes 60 primary sites that were repeatedly sampled across campaigns, as well as 10 additional named locations visited in a single campaign. The resulting catalogue contains 2,550 sample records, including 1,271 samples with amplicon sequencing data. Our resource links the samples to field environmental observations, pH measurements, X-ray fluorescence (XRF) measurements, and climate data.

To improve interoperability and enable integrated queries across heterogeneous data sources, we represent the resource using established ontologies and standardized identifiers. We represnt sites, samples, processes, qualities, and measurement values using the Semanticscience Integrated Ontology (SIO) \cite{sio}. This representation explicitly links sequencing results and environmental measurements to the samples and collection events they derive from, retaining their connections to the underlying data-generation processes. We encode chemical entities with ChEBI \cite{chebi}, environmental classifications with ENVO \cite{envo}, and taxonomic identifiers with NCBI Taxonomy \cite{ncbitaxon}. The resulting linked RDF modules support queries across sampling campaigns, samples, microbial communities, and environmental observations while preserving the context in which each observation was generated.

We performed technical validation to assess the consistency and integrity of the resource, including reconcilation with source data, measurement inclusion, identifier mappings, and structural constraints. We also tested executable queries to demonstrate how information can be retrieved across linked datasets. We report the scope of each validation separately, including the ontology constructs and moducles processed by the ELK reasoner \cite{elk}. The portal (\url{https://rubalkhali.science/}) provides site-level pages and a SPARQL endpoint for querying the resource. The Data Records section describes the available datasets, and Data Availability specifies their access conditions. A companion manuscript presents analyses of microbial community composition and environmental associations \cite{ecology_companion}, while this Data Descriptor documents the underlying data, their representation, and the provisions supporting their reuse.
